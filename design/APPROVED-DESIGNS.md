# THE SIGNED PAGE
## The eleven screens, his seals, and what is still owed

**The work** `design/` — the UI and UX of LAHARĪ, built from *Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu* and from the persona charter in `RUPAGOSWAMI-PM.md`
**The approver** Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī (persona), sole owner and sole signatory
**The designer** who is not named in his letters and does not sign the page
**Dates** foundations sealed Day 50 of his ninety; screens sealed Days 51–53. ISO record: 2026-11-06.

---

## 1. What this page is

He does not approve a document by nodding at it. He approves it by writing three lines and a seal, in the margin, in ink. This page collects those seals in one place so that a reader can see, without opening ten thousand lines, what was signed and what was not.

A release without a signed page did not happen. That is his rule, and this is the page.

**It is not a summary of the design.** The design is in the eleven documents. This is only the record of who put his name to them and what he refused.

The standard he set in that last letter is the standard the eleven screens were then held to, and it is quoted at the head of every one of them:

> **Every screen is held to the first word that does not fit: a broken word returns the screen; a moved column must show its walk.**

Four documents stand on that seal, 3,228 lines: `01-FOUNDATIONS.md` (colour, type, the margin, the grid, motion, identity), `02-COMPONENTS.md` (seventeen parts and what each refuses), `03-VOICE.md` (every permitted and every forbidden string), `04-ACCESSIBILITY.md` (his eyes, his hand, his old phone, his grief). `INTERLOCKS.md` holds the seven invariants they must not disagree about.

## 4. What he changed, in his own decisions

These are the places where his reading overruled the designer's drawing. They are the most useful pages in the corpus, because each one is a refusal that cost something.

| The designer drew | He ruled | Where it landed |
|---|---|---|
| One photograph in the product, of a lamp | **Two**: the form is the subject of Darśana, the lamp is the second photograph | S-04 |
| The reader's one act was *Where this comes from* | The reader's limb is hearing: the act is *Hear it*, and the chain is a sentence at the foot | S-05 |
| A bell on ekādaśī night saying that nothing will ring | Struck. A bell whose message is that no bell will come is *prajalpa* with a sound on it | 03 |
| Eight arcs of unequal length, as a clock face | Eight equal arcs. The unequal ones were the clock to the decimal — a tally in geometry | S-02, 01 |
| A 26pt verse on the reader, to make it fit | 30/42 and scroll. A line is not made smaller to make a verse fit | S-05, 01 |
| The last line at 5.86, dimmed | 8.73 by day, `--moon` at night. The last line is the only thing he takes out of the room | S-10, 04 |
| The way out as a vertical strip in the faintest ink | Horizontal, 18pt, in `--ink`, 88 wide, at the thumb | all |
| 60-character bell bodies | 54, then derived: two lines at 27 characters on the narrowest lock screen | 03 |
| Two night inks | Three. Night secondary text at 4.94 is 3.46 through a replaced lens, and the corpus was breaking its own rule in five places | 01, 02, 04 |
| Five questions, weekly | Four, and no week. A question can become a rule, and a rule is *niyamāgraha* | S-08, spec F10 |

Two he conceded, because the designer's arithmetic was right and his was not: the address of the word *reviewer unnamed* (the margin cannot hold it), and the night's floor for the last line. He wrote, in his own hand, *you were right twice* — and then returned the corpus anyway, for a different fault.

## 5. The refusals issued during the work

Two, both permanent, both from the book and not from taste.

**The microphone that keeps time by the voice.** Refused on Day 47. *Anubhāva* is the response of the one who loves, offered; it is not a signal for the room to receive. A machine that waits on a man's voice to know when to sing again has begun to listen to him. Kīrtana keeps time by the hand.

**The camera.** Refused before the work began, and never re-opened. There is no camera permission in the product, so there is nothing to detect tears with, and *sāttvika-bhāvas* remain unreachable by any sensor. The refusal is cheaper than a policy.

A third refusal was considered and refused: the prototype. A drawn specimen that cannot be opened by the person it is for teaches the team to trust a picture. The screens are drawings and specifications; the first build is the prototype.

## 7. What is owed to the build

He sealed the screens. He did not pretend the corpus is finished, and neither does this page.

The live version of this table is `OPEN-BEFORE-BUILD.md`, which carries the same debts one by one, together with the two sittings he demanded and the six duplicated facts closed on Day 54.

| Owed | Whose | Why it is not closed here |
|---|---|---|
| A named translator and a named reviewer | Content, not design | Every chain in the corpus prints *translator unnamed · reviewer unnamed*, because no person has signed. He would rather print the absence than a name that was never asked |
| The door's width at 200% on both frames | 01, 02, 04, I4 | Three pages carry 88, 96 and 104; the arithmetic is settled and the sentence is not yet one sentence |
| The seal's size at 300% | 02 §11, 04 A5 | 18 × 18 and 24 × 24 stand in two pages; S-09 places the square and refuses to assert its size |
| The quiet page behind the door | S-10, 04 §4.7 | He has not ruled whether a page may stand there, and the designer will not invent the ruling |
| Two arithmetic shortfalls on S-06 | S-06 | The room's name at 200% and 300% and the window word at 200% were named in the seal as owed, not sealed over |
| A seventh frame for S-11 | S-11 | The day's empty floor is stated and measured but not drawn |
| A sitting with a sādhaka | S-01, S-03 | *Bring me the person* was used twice. The corpus records whom he wanted and what he would ask; the sitting has not happened |

These are written here because a signed page that hides its own debts is a page he would take back.

## 8. The record, honestly

The corpus was built over five days of his calendar by many hands, and it was returned four times at the foundation and twice on eight screens. The last pass — reconciling every page to every other — was not finished when the work stopped. What is in `design/` is therefore **eleven sealed screens and a grammar that is true in its rules and not yet identical in every sentence.** The remaining disagreements are listed in §7, not hidden.

He would say that this is the difference between a temple and a drawing of one: the drawing can be finished, and the temple cannot.

## 9. The signature

> I have read eleven screens and four documents, and I have held each at arm's length, and I have returned more of them than I signed. What is signed is signed.
>
> They are not beautiful. They are fitting, which is the harder thing and the one I asked for. A man can arrive at the threshold without an account, hear one verse, and leave; and nothing in this product will ask him for anything he did not come to give.
>
> Two things I will watch in the build, and they are the two I have watched since the first morning: whether the ring becomes a score by accident, and whether the last screen still says one thing when the team is tired.
>
> I may be wrong about the margin. I have been wrong before, and I said so twice in these pages, which is cheaper than defending a sentence I wrote at dusk.
>
> — *Rūpa*

**Sealed** Day 53. **Countersigned** in the repository by the designer, who does not sign the page and keeps the letters.
