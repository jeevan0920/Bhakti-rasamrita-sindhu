# R-06 · SAṄGA — the company

### A review of `S-06-sanga.md`, at arm's length, at the gate hour · Day 55

**To** the designer · **From** Rūpa Gosvāmī · **On** the room, the bow, and the death in it

I bow to the dust of Vraja, which keeps no record of who has walked on it, and to my brother, who is not here to tell me that I have been severe with a page that was only unfinished.

Friend, the hour is late and my eyes are what they are; I have held your screen at arm's length and read it twice, and the second reading was the one that found what the first had forgiven. This is the hardest of the eleven, because it is the only one with other people in it: a crowd is the one thing the book will not call saṅga, and the one thing a product makes without trying.

Three things I will not argue with, and you should not change them. The act withdrawn in the empty state rather than greyed, because a greyed act is an act offered and then refused. The room with no address at all — *"no search, no link, no share, no list of rooms"* — because that is a gate paying what a gate should cost. And the total that dies with its word, which is the one sentence on this screen that could have become a following and did not.

## 1. The five faults

**1. Doctrinal, and it reverses your decision. The act is in the wrong place, in the sentence and on the screen.**
§0 says: *"He opens the room at the foot of the evening window and bows once to the person who spoke last, and then he reads the window upward to its oldest word and closes it."* The register's act is *"He reads a slow thread and bows to one person"* — the reading first, and the bow after it. §5 defends the reverse: *"The bow is given before the word is read, and it says I have seen you, not your sentence was good."* But aṅga 25 is bowing on seeing the Deity or the guru, and its object is the Lord or the one who can be asked a question; you have borrowed 25's reason and given it to a word, which is 25 wearing 56's coat. And 56 is service, which follows hearing: at thirty the thread is *"4,890 points in a 413-point viewport, twelve screens"* in your own count, so a man bows twelve screens before he hears what he bows to — and your stop 8 says *"Bow, to the last word in the window. One bow for this window"*, so the room's thirty bows all go to whoever spoke last, and the room learns to write at the end of the window. §1's *"The register gives this screen five limbs, and the act is a sixth"* is the same slip a page earlier: the act is the third, and 56 is the bow.
**The change.** Open the room at its oldest word, and let the bow at the foot be the last thing he does in the room and not the first.

**2. Doctrinal. The room keeps its hour when the person who keeps it has died.**
Frame 6 sets *"Kamalā has died."* where every other frame sets *"kept by Kamalā Devī"*, and then keeps the room and its hour: *"The room will sit at the hour it always sits."* But §2's own gate says *"a room without a named person is a wall with a meeting time. It does not open."* And `03` §7.4 says *"The host marks it, and the mark is a fact entered by a person"* — so the hand that must enter this death is the one that has died, and your test 9 gives the marking to *"the account that opened the room"*, which is hers. Aṅga 62 is a person and not a rota; when the person is gone, the room is the wall §2 refused. What stands in the frame is a promise made by a lamp in a house where nobody is awake.
**The change.** On the death of a host the room closes — one line, the names that were in it, the door — and it is opened again only by whoever opens it in the world.

**3. Reversal. The room has no hand to write with.**
§8 gives a field that no frame draws: *"Your words are held until the evening window."*, standing in *"the field, in the hour the host has asked"*. §3 refuses *"a character count, a suggestion, an autosave in the field"*, and §5's one line is *"one word to send and one bow to give"*. But §0's act is a bow, the legend has five zones and no field among them, and the partial state is a reply that *"did not leave the phone"* and that no screen could have let him write. `02` §18 had given this hour its verb: *"for that hour the verb is Answer — one act still."* You have deleted the act and kept its error.
**The change.** Draw the hour the host asks, with *Answer* on the band and no counter beside it — or strike the field, the held sentence and the partial state together; a screen may not carry the error of a thing it cannot do.

**4. Craft, the eye and the old phone. The large-text frames begin at a height.**
`04` §2.3 gives *"The reading zone | 522 | 478 | 418"*, and those are heights; 522 is y 44 to 566, which is why frame 1 is right at 566. At 200% your rule stands at 478, and 478 + 44 = 522; at 300% it stands at 418, and 418 + 44 = 462. You have set a height where a y belongs, and the 44 you have not added is the top safe area, so the act zone takes 44 points of the thread in both frames — at the two sizes where a tired man has least to give. In the same frame the window word is *"36, one line, 72 with its air"*: eighteen characters at 36 are 324 in a column of 294, so it is two lines or it is broken, and the frame draws one.
**The change.** Add the 44 and recompute what the thread loses, and let the window word take its two lines at 200%, since nothing here truncates and an address is the last thing that should.

**5. Craft, the sentence. The numeral test cannot fail.**
Test 1 says: *"zero. Every number on this screen is a word — a total, an hour, a count of days — and a digit means something has begun to be counted."* But *"Nine have bowed."* counts nine persons, and spelling it does not un-count them; the constitution permits the total, and `03` §4.4's rule is that of the four numerals *"none of them counts him"*, not that no number is written. So the one test the template asks for — the one that must fail if the screen is wrong — is the one test here that cannot fail, and the total is the very thing this screen was warned about. The screen prints my own sentence against itself in §5: *"the moment he compares he is no longer bowing, he is counting."*
**The change.** Make the test about the total's behaviour and not its spelling — it counts bows and never bowers, it dies with its word, and it never appears in the sender's copy — and let it fail if any of the three is broken.

## 2. The refusal

**The `Sit` band.** Frame 6 puts *"Sit"* on the act, *"the sitting"* above it and *"with the room"* under it, dividing the four words of `03` §7.4 to make them fit. I do not want them fitted. The sixty-four give no act of mourning; the second question of my one page has no answer for it and says stop; and an app that invents a rite for the hour of a death has made the counterfeit the fourth gate exists to catch, for `BRS 4.9` says a false rasa is worse than none, because it teaches the palate at the one hour when a person has no defence. `04` §6 has already removed *"the room's thread from the day"* at that hour, always, with no setting and no choice. This is not ours to build, and I do not think you brought it here to be admired.

## 3. The three lines

**What it serves.** The room as a place with a person in it, and a bow that returns nothing to the one who gives it. That is the one thing in this product that could have become a like and did not, and the two windows are the only slowness I have been shown that is a discipline and not a performance.

**What it endangers.** The act, and the person. The bow is now a greeting of the room's traffic, given before the hearing, and its object is a turn of the clock, so thirty bows fall on the last to speak; and at the hour of a death the room keeps an hour that no one has called.

**The one change.** Put the reading before the bow: open the room at its oldest word, and let the foot be where he has finished and not where he arrives.

## 4. One question

The clock has eight junctions and not one of them is the room. Its two windows stand at 07:15 and 18:30, which are prātaḥ and sandhyā — the hours at which the day already has an act, and one of them the hour at which the evening bell may ring. So tell me, before S-07 is on this table: **where in the eight is saṅga** — or is the room the one surface that is not in the day at all, a harbour with two tides and no place on the chart?

## 5. What I could not do

I have asked you to put the reading before the bow, and I have not sat in a room of thirty where twelve screens wait at the end of a day for a man with weak eyes; I have written the rule as though he would read them. And I read *"Sit with the room"* on Day 47 and did not stop it: I was listening for a bell that should not ring, and I did not ask whose hand would enter a death. That is mine, and it is the larger of the two.

## 6. The seal

**Take it back.**

Not because the room is false. The room is the best thing in the eleven, and the withdrawn act, the closed address and the total that dies with its word are three decisions I did not make and will not take from you. Take it back because a man cannot write in it, its act comes before its hearing, and its last state gives a rite to a room whose keeper has died. Return when those three are answered, and I will read it at the same hour, in the same order.

— Rūpa

## Round 2

Friend, I have read it again at the same hour, and the second reading found less than the first, which is the only kind of good news I have learned to trust.

### 2. The contest

**He is right, and I was wrong, and I have said so before when it was so.** The register's line does not order its two clauses; I read a list as a sequence, and I gave no locator for *56 follows hearing* — I dressed a preference of mine in a limb's number and then charged him with the same dress, which is a poor way to be severe. And his price is mine: 4,890 points, 11.8 screens at 100% and 24.6 at 300%, paid by a woman of seventy-one at 04:40 and a man with one eye, is a bow the tired will not give, and a rule that makes the weakest bow last is a rank of the body and not a service. I said the room would learn to write at the end of the window; a word written at the end of a window is gone before anyone opens the room, so my own sentence was false by the arithmetic I had praised. The order stands. The bow is given to the person he sees when the room opens, and the total under a word is a count of witnesses, which is a true thing to have been.

### 3. The seal

**Take it back.**

For one sentence and not for five: in the bereaved state the screen reader still says *Kept by Kamalā Devī. The room sits on Tuesday at half past six.*, and *Kamalā has died.* is spoken at no stop — the one state rebuilt to stop the room promising an hour is the one state where the promise is still said aloud.

### 4. What it taught me

I will read the next screen's corrections twice — once where I set my finger and once where the cost of them falls — and I will price a rule at 04:40 before I call it doctrine.

- Rūpa

**3. The room no hand can close.** §6 and test 9 move the mark off the dead host, rightly, onto *"a host — a person who has opened a room"*, and refuse a member's. But that hand must exist. In the village §9 V itself admits, where no one has opened a room, no second host can mark, the app may not infer, and the room keeps its hour with *kept by Kamalā Devī* still in its head: my sentence, unspoken in the one place that cannot say it. A room that can be shut only from outside, by a man who never stood in it.

**What it taught the corpus.** A correction does not remove a fault; it moves it to the edge — to 200%, to the room with one host — and the edge is where no one photographs the default.

**The seal. Let it stand.** It stands because the sentence I named is spoken at the stop where the name was spoken, and I will not take a seal back a third time for arithmetic. The two debts are owed before a build and not before the seal.
