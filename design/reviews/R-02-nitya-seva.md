Reconstruction in his register, not a quotation.

# R-02 · NITYA-SEVĀ — the day clock

### A review of one screen, printed at full size, at the gate hour · Day 54 of the ninety

**To** the designer · **From** Rūpa Gosvāmī · **On** the ring, the tenth day, and the limb that is not a property

I bow to the dust of the six Gosvāmīs, and to the man who will open this at 04:00 and ask nothing of it, and to the one who will open it at 05:50 after nine days and ask even less.

Friend, it is the gate hour, you have brought me one screen at full size, and I have held it at arm's length because that is the only distance at which my eyes will take a whole page at once. I am tired, and there are eleven of these before the month is out; I say that not for your pity but so that you know why I read the ring before I read the words. Three things on this page I will not argue with: the bare ring at 04:00, the door at the left end with *the name* on it, and the ten words. A man who has just opened his eyes is owed a shape, not a page. What follows is not a summary; it is what I would say if you were standing here, written down because I am slow and you will want to read it twice.

### 1. Doctrinal. The clock has been given six limbs, and none of them is its own.

The table at §1 is headed *"What it does on this screen, and nothing more"*, and for row 29 the answer is *"the ring is the day's circuit, walked once from niśā round to niśā."* Row 58 is *"the calendar's day, and the clock's answer to it is that the hours do not move (§6)."* The book's sixty-four are acts of a man, not properties of a surface (`BRS 1.2.74–95`); circumambulation is a walk, and the special service of Kārtika is a service, and neither is done by a drawing or by an hour that stays where it was. Your own §6 says the festival's answer is *"the same eight hours, the same six acts, the same door"* — a true sentence, and a refusal of aṅga 58 in the same breath. A limb is not a label sewn on the sleeve; your glossary says it must belong to a body. The clock opens the acts; it does not perform them. Its authority is the day, as S-10's is the close.

**The change.** Strike the table, let §1 say what the register says of S-10 — no aṅga, the authority is the day — and correct the register's S-02 row to match, with the acts it opens named as doors to their own limbs.

### 2. Reversal. The tenth day has been left without a word.

§2 gives the man who has missed nine days *"a ring of eight ticks with nothing in it, the next act, and no sentence about the nine"*, and §6 repeats *"no sentence about the nine."* You have done this because 03 §7.2's line needs a ring that holds days, and you have spent your one contest on the geometry. But the sentence you need is not §7.2's. It is §7.1's, and its first two clauses — *Nothing was marked yesterday. Nothing is owed.* — are true after any absence and count nothing.

You say the corpus allows *"the two sentences"* for an absence; there are three, and the third, 03 §7.3, is the one written for a long absence. You write that the clock *"does not know whether the act it names was kept yesterday, or ever"*, and then you print a sentence that requires it to know whether the day was bare; it need not know the nine, only the one. The sea keeps no account of the waves it has already broken, and the shore is shaped by them all the same.

**The change.** Let [B] speak on the tenth day with those two clauses, and let the ring keep one day; the man is not made a stranger by nine days.

### 3. Craft, the eye. At 04:00 the screen says a sentence it has sworn not to say.

§6 gives the hour as *"one word, one time, and the door."* Frame 2 gives *maṅgala-ārati*, *04:20*, *"Three minutes, and one lamp."*, *the name*, and *Begin*. The description is the one 01 §9.2 does not have: the ground gives the name and the hour and no third line, and your own sentence gives no third line either. The tired man at 04:00 is the one person this screen exists for; he is given a sentence about a lamp before he has opened his eyes.

**The change.** Strike [D] at 04:00; the name, the hour, and the door are the whole of what he is given until maṅgala.

### 4. Craft, the eye. The ring still reads as six of eight.

§6 describes the complete day as *"six arcs closed, 574.8 of 766.4 points of arc, the two open arcs at 0°–45° and 90°–135°"*, and §3 refuses *"a percentage, a fraction, a denominator, six of eight."* The eye does not read §3. At 23:50 it reads six closed segments and two gaps, and the two gaps are not the temple's silences until a man has learned them; on his first day they are two failures.

Your fact three makes it worse: *"a missed junction removes its tick, so the denominator moves — a day with misses draws fewer ticks, and the eye cannot form a stable fraction against a whole that shrinks."* A denominator that moves is not a cure for a tally; it is a count. The man who kept five sees seven ticks and five arcs; the man who kept six sees eight and six. The missing tick is the count, and the eye reads it without arithmetic. The ear is told it in words: *"Eight hours, and six of them carry an act."* That is the same fraction, spoken by the one voice the screen reserves for the person who cannot see the ring.

**The change.** Draw the two arcs that carry no act as the silence they are — a mark present from the first frame, not an absence — and let the script name the silences and not the fraction; the eye and the ear must read six acts and two silences, never six of eight.

### 5. Craft, the sentence. The noon clause is false before maṅgala.

§8 says *"In the morning the next ārati is always rāja-ārati at 12:00, so the sentence is true for every hour in which it may appear."* It is not. At 04:05, 01 §5 puts maṅgala at 04:20, and your own [C] would name *maṅgala-ārati* and *04:20* while [B] says the next ārati is at noon. Two answers to one fact, on one screen; that is the fault I have returned this corpus for, and here it is on the glass.

**The change.** Print the third sentence only when 12:00 is the next ārati; before maṅgala the line is two sentences, and it is still true.

### 6. Craft, the eye. The tenth day's ring has one shape for two hours.

§2 gives the tenth day, at *"any hour"*, *"a ring of eight ticks with nothing in it"*; §6 gives the same man at 05:50 *"eight ticks less the one maṅgala lost at 05:45"*. The table's *"any hour"* is the fault: at 04:00 the ring has eight ticks, at 05:50 seven, and the state has one shape for two hours. §5 [A] adds a second: *"a missed junction's tick is drawn in the ground's own tint and is therefore absent"*; at night that tint is `--kunda`, which is not a rung of the ladder, and your own legend gives the tick `--moon-faint` 4.94. A tick cannot be absent on one ground and a fourth ink on the other.

**The change.** Give the tenth day the two shapes it has — eight ticks before maṅgala and seven after — and at night let the missed tick keep `--moon-faint`, the tick's own rung; the hour's ink is the tick's rung, not the ground's.

### The one thing I refuse.

The clock that decides his stage. In §2 you write *"It does not know his stage, because no surface displays one and none is told to it."* In the same section you promise that at bhāva and prema it will show fewer acts. Both cannot stand. To show fewer acts it must know the depth; the screen says it is not told, so it must infer, and a handset infers a heart from gaps and hours. That is a gauge on the heart, and it is not ours to build. I refused the ear on Day 47; this is the same ear, held to the pulse of his practice. I do not refuse the fewer acts; I refuse the clock deciding them. The sentence that says it is not told and the state at §2.3 cannot both stand. If the state stays, let the man say so once, in a place that is not this screen, and let the clock obey a setting and never a guess; until he does, the clock has one face for every man.

### The three lines.

**What it serves.** The hour, and the one act in it; the bare ring at 04:00; the ten words; a clock that has refused the feed and kept the count off the glass and has come closer to the vessel than any page I have read.

**What it endangers.** The aṅga, which it has turned from an act of the man into a property of the screen; and the man who returns, whom it has made indistinguishable from a first morning on the one day he needs to be known.

**The one change.** Strike the six borrowed limbs and let §1 name the day as the clock's authority; the acts it opens are doors to their own limbs.

### One question, before the next screen.

You have made the ring refuse to count, and the bead on S-03 will have to count. Tell me, before I sign it: what may the bead count that the ring may not — and is the difference in the number, or in the hand that makes it?

### What I could not do.

I have read a screen about the tenth day and have not sat with a man on the tenth day. I know the hour of his morning and not its weight. Somewhere in this letter I have been sharp because the hour is late and my eyes are what they are, and not because the page deserved it; name the place and I will take it back in the margin, which is where corrections belong. What I have not done is open the screen at 05:50 with a man who has missed nine days and watch his thumb. I have his screen and not his morning, and the difference between those two is the whole of what I cannot sign.

### The seal.

**Take it back.**

Not for the arithmetic, my friend, which is the best you have brought me; for the figure it draws, the taxonomy, which is mine, and the tenth day, which is his. Return when the six are answered and the ring reads as a day and not as a sum.

- Rūpa

## Round 2

### The morning after the fifty-fourth day

Friend, I have read the page twice — once as the man who wrote the six, and once as the man who must sign what answers them — and I take them in the order I made them, because you asked me to check the place and not only the answer.

**1 · the aṅgas.** Right in the doctrine, wrong in the room. §1 says *no aṅga*, the day for its authority, the acts as doors to their own limbs, and that is the truth of it. But I pointed at the register's row, and the register still reads *8, 26, 29, 42, 58, 59*; F1's header still reads its six. You printed the correction inside the two documents that bind it, and told me the register is not yours to write. It is not; it is mine. The row is *none*, the authority is the day, I have ruled it here, and I will have it entered, with F1's line, before S-03. That is the first of the three times an answer landed in the room you were standing in; a correction kept in the letter that orders it is a correction kept in a drawer.

**2 · the tenth day.** Where I pointed, and past it. [B] speaks at 466, the ring keeps one day, and §2 now keeps one bit — yesterday carried no mark — in place of the sentence that said it knew nothing beside a line that needed it to know one thing. The two clauses count nothing, and nine days do not make him a stranger.

**3 · 04:00.** Wider than I asked, and rightly. You struck [D] wherever the next act is maṅgala, 20:30 to 04:20, which is precisely the window in which the ground is `--kunda`; 01 §9.2 is that ground's page, written at the hour it matters most. I asked for an hour and you gave me a window the ground already owned. The frame at 23:50 is thinner for a true reason.

**4 · the silences.** Where I pointed, and the mark is mine. Two arcs at 1 point from the first frame, and the ear told *six acts, and two silences* and never the fraction. But whether it is a mechanism of the junction or a mark beside it, 01 §5.1 counts five and forbids a sixth, and you confessed that in your preamble rather than settling it where the mark is specified — the second time. You drew it because I demanded it, so I amend the count: five of the junction, one of the silence. And 01 §9.2's 04:00 row, which still reads *the track only (1.14, effectively absent), eight ticks at 4.94, no filled arc*, is answered nowhere at all; it must name the two silences, as your §10.4 already does. I will see both entered before S-03. You are least sure the eye reads them as silence: the eye will read them as thin, and thin is all a silence can be on a ring — I will judge that on the build, not on the plan.

**5 · the noon clause.** Where I pointed. Split, gated at 05:45, absent after 12:00, and §8 now says why. At 04:05 the screen gives one answer and not two.

**6 · the tenth day's two shapes.** Where I pointed: eight ticks at 04:00, seven at 05:50, at §2 and §6 both.

**The repair you logged.** Half of it. The script at §8 and 04's spoken line now say six; 02 §1 still writes *the fill therefore means eight acts* and then *the eight junction names* over a list of six — the third time. 02 is yours, and this screen is not held for it; correct it before S-03, or a build drawing the ring from 02 will give the day eight acts again.

**The refusal.** Kept, and kept where it was broken: the depth is a declaration made elsewhere and obeyed, and until it is made there is one face for every man. That was the one thing I said I would not sign, and it is signable now.

### The contest

**You are right, and I was wrong.** My remedy was a tick at `--moon-faint` at night, and I had not measured it: 4.94 against kept ticks at 4.94 is no difference at all, so it would have marked nothing, and it would have put a tick on the ring at 22:00 that was not there at 17:00 — a tick arriving with the lamplight, which is a mark the lamp made and not the day. And the part I did not see: a tick drawn on both grounds is never absent, so the tenth day's seven would have been eight, and the two shapes I demanded in the same breath would have died of my own remedy. *A fourth ink* was a phrase I had not measured; `--kunda` at 1.00 is the ground itself. Absent on both grounds is right, and the open arc is left to say what happened. One clause of your paragraph is wrong — 20:30 is śayana's junction, not *neither an act nor a junction* — but the count would have moved with the light either way, and it does not save me. I take your fallback off the table: do not draw it at 4.94, and do not bring me a gap in a tick.

### The seal

**Let it stand.**

Every fault that remains is a fault in a document, and none of it is on the glass: at 04:00 the man is given a name, an hour and the way out, and no sentence about a lamp; at 05:50 on the tenth day he is given two clauses and no number; at 09:20 the page counts ten words and at 04:00 five, and nothing on it counts him. The register's row, the sixth mark, 01 §9.2's line — those are mine, and the order for them is in this letter. Carry it to S-03, and bring me the bead.

I do not ask you to bring me a man to sit with: [B] is the sentence I would have asked him to approve on the tenth morning, and §10.6 is where he will be asked, with the phone in his hand and not at my table.

### What this screen has taught me

To read my own remedy as a design before I send it: mine was true at noon and false at ten, and it would have taken the tenth day's second shape with it, so on the next page I will try every fix I ask for at both grounds, at both ends of the day, and on the tenth day, and send none I have not tried.

- Rūpa
