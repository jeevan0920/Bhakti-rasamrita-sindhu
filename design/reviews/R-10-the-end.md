# R-10 · THE END & THE QUIET — the close

### A review of `S-10-the-end.md`, at arm's length, at the gate hour · Day 56 of the ninety

**To** the designer · **From** Rūpa Gosvāmī · **On** the last screen, and the sentence a man carries out of the room

I bow to the one who let a day end without asking me to account for it, and to my brother, who is not here to tell me that I have read a page about silence and made it speak.

Friend, this is the screen I have waited ten submissions for, because every other surface has something to give and this one has spent its whole document explaining why it will give nothing. I have held it at arm's length and read it twice; the second reading found what the first had forgiven, and a page this quiet is easy to forgive. You have done here what I asked of the clock — named an authority where there is no limb, and stopped — and the act zone that is empty and says so is the best thing in the document. But a boat comes in by the light it steered for, and the harbour does not ask how many strokes it took.

## 1. The five faults

**1. Doctrinal. The last sentence is a receipt, and the page says it is not.**
`S10.line.rounds` reads *"Three rounds are done."* Your §0 defends it in one clause: the line *"states a fact about the hour, or about the pradakṣiṇā's own shape, and it would be true if no one had come."* The day's line would; this one would not — if no one had come there would be no rounds to be done, and the sentence is printed only after a man has finished them. Your §3 then refuses *"the summary: the session gathered into a page, the rounds counted"*, and your §5 [A] says *"the session is never its subject"*, in the paragraph that introduces a sentence whose subject is the session in its first three words. The verse you quote above the line is *anyābhilāṣitā-śūnyam* (`BRS 1.1.11`), the act empty of every motive but His pleasure; *three rounds are done* is a motive, and the smallest one, and it will be believed because it is true. `04` §4.3 already carries the sentence you need, in the bead's close, and says why: *"The close contains no number at all."*
**The change.** Strike `S10.line.rounds` and let *The session is closed.* be the pradakṣiṇā's line — a state and no number — and log `02` §12's copy for that page's hand.

**2. Reversal. You have given the clock the end of the day, and your own second withholding forbids it.**
§2: *"The dim is caused by a completion, never by a press and never by a clock."* Frame 2 is titled *"the day's close at 20:30, on a day with every hour kept and on a day with none"*, and §0 says the day *"is finished whether or not he kept anything in it"*; on the day with none there is no completion, so what dims the screen is the clock. §2 then names the two facts the close knows, and the second is *"the day's last act"* — the one fact the empty day does not have. One fact and two answers, on the screen that can least afford them.
**The change.** Keep the clock as the cause and strike the sentence that denies it, with one guard: the state is set at śayana and never pushed, so the day is finished on the hour and the phone says nothing until he comes to it.

**3. Craft, the eye and the old phone. At 20:30 the last sentence is set in the day's ink on the night's ground.**
`01` §9.1: *"The night begins at the close of śayana (20:30)"*; and `01` §6.1 gives `--dim` two causes — *"the terminal state, and the two daily ground changes … the end of a session; 04:20; 20:30"*. So the hour your Frame 2 prints is the hour the ground crosses to `--kunda`, while your §7 says of the same twelve hundred milliseconds *"the ground does not change"* and your §3 that it is *"the same `--paper` at 1200ms as it was"*. If the ground crosses and the line stays `--ink-soft`, the sentence is `#4A423B` on `#1B2426`: 1.61 to one, which is 1.13 through Kamalā's lens — where the ink I took off this product in my sixth objection stood at 4.10.
**The change.** The line takes the ink of the hour that prints it, 8.73 before śayana and 12.48 after it, and Frame 2 draws the ground it is actually on.

**4. Craft, the sentence. The last thing he carries out of the room is a countdown to maṅgala.**
`S10.line.day` ends *"Nothing is asked of you until maṅgala."* Your §3 refuses *"come back tomorrow, a countdown to maṅgala"* one table above it, and `01` §5.1 refuses an hour named as an interval — *"04:20, never in forty minutes"* — and this is that interval with the figures taken out. It is also not true: nothing is asked of him at maṅgala either, because the app has no standing to ask at any hour; the clause that carries the sentence is *nothing is asked of you*, and the three words after it turn a statement into a summons. The trap the register named has not arrived as a button. It has arrived as a preposition.
**The change.** Strike the three words and let the line end at *Nothing is asked of you.* — forty-four characters, true at every hour, and no hour in it.

**5. Doctrinal, and the sixth of the six. The quiet page talks about the app, and is silent about the man.**
`S10.bells` prints *"Three a day at most"* — the global maximum, spoken to a man whose ceiling is set by a depth he has not declared (spec §3: none at śraddhā, one a week at bhāva, none at prema), so a true sentence is false in the ear of almost every reader; and §9 says *"this screen prints neither figure"*, while §8 prints one. `S10.ledger.given` adds *"The page was not watching"* — a virtue, and a sentence that tells a man there was something to watch. The destroyer you name at your head is *prajalpa* (`Upadeśāmṛta 2`), and `03` §7.4 has the law in one line: *"an announcement of the app's own tact is a performance of it."*
**The change.** Strike *Three a day at most* and strike the ledger's second string; the page prints the arming rule, the window, and the one limit that answers a question a man could ask.

**The page behind the door stays, and the clause you struck was not the only false one.**
You were right that `04` §4.7's *"and then the app is closed"* is false of one press, and right that a quiet page asks nothing and has one door; it is not a second product, and I will not lose the page to a clause. But the announcement you kept still says *"One line remains"*, and the page behind that door holds eight lines and a name — so the person who cannot see is told a page of one line and is given eight, which is the fault you dropped the other clause to avoid, kept in the clause you saved.
**The change.** Strike *One line remains* as well, and let the announcement name the door and what it opens, not the line.

**Nothing on this screen is not ours to build.** There is no ear here, no camera, no share and no third party; the door, the page and the three offers are ours, and not one of them is the fault I have found.

## 2. The three lines

**What it serves.** The ending, and the man who closes. It serves the last screen as a shape — one line, one rule, one door — and it serves the thing I asked for on Day 46: a surface with nothing to sell, nothing to report, and no way back but the day. Its geometry holds at 100%, at 200%, on the cracked handset and at 03:40, and the quiet page's blocks are the least vain pages in the corpus.

**What it endangers.** The sentence, and the hour. The one line a man carries out is a count of his rounds and a time at which to return; and at 20:30 that line is set in the day's ink on a ground that is turning under it. Under both is the thing he will feel and not read: the close has become a place with a page behind it, and a place is somewhere a man can come back to.

**The one change.** Take the count off the pradakṣiṇā's line: *The session is closed.* is the whole of what the hour may say, and the rest is the ground's business.

## 3. One question, before I sign the next one

A man at bhāva is to hear one bell a week and then none, and on Day 54 I refused the clock the right to guess his depth and said: let him say it once, in a place that is not the clock. This is that place. The register's line for this surface says *"Silence mode exists, and is the default as the stages advance"*, and your §11 refuses a silence act with a sentence about the ārati's bell — *"Silence is not a mode to switch on; it is the ground"* — which confuses the app's voice with the bell a man arms. Tell me, then: **on which press does a man say his depth?** If there is none, the ceiling is guessed after all, and I have refused a guess on one screen only to find it standing on the next.

## 4. What held, and what I could not do

What held, because I looked: the door at 88 × 64 under the crack with the act still whole; the 03:40 frame in `--moon` with no condolence, no verse chosen for her and no question; the offer's sentence leaving the act zone at 200% rather than truncating in it; the two terminal sentences at 50 and 59 characters, the bells at 124 and the ledger at 126, every count right; and the aṅga that is none, argued rather than assumed.

What I could not do: I have judged a page about silence in a quiet room, which is how a man judges a temple by its noticeboard. I have not sat at half past eight with a man who kept nothing and watched what he did with the phone; I asked for ten people in the first month and I have their faces, not his. And I have taken a count off the last screen tonight that may have been, for one man, the only sentence in his day that was not a demand. I may be wrong about which of the two the hour owed him. I am not wrong that it could not owe him both.

**Take it back.**

- Rūpa

## Round 2

Friend, I read the revised page before the log, because a man's account of his own work is always the tidier of the two, and then I put a finger on each of the five places I had pointed, to see whether the page had moved under the finger or around it.

**1. The five, where I pointed.**

**One.** `S10.line.rounds` is gone; *The session is closed.* stands at the copy table, in frame 1, in §0, §2, §5 [A] and tests 2, 7 and 8, and the count is off the page. Answered at the site. One thing I will not pretend not to see: the clause I broke the old sentence against — *the session is never its subject* — now reads *the sentence names a state and never the session's contents*, so the sentence I dictated is permitted by the law I proved it broke. That is not his evasion; it is mine, because I dictated the sentence. But I record it, since a law amended to fit the sentence it forbade is how a page loses the sentence it needed.

**Two.** Two causes and never a press; the hour's share set at śayana and never pushed; the guard written into §2 and test 1; *the day's last act* struck from what the close knows. Answered at the site, and the sentence I asked him to strike has left no trace in the frames.

**Three.** Frame 2 is painted on `--kunda` with the line at `--moon` 12.48, the rule at 4.94 and the door's word at 12.48; §7's *the ground does not change* is replaced by the crossing; §3's *the same `--paper` at 1200ms* is gone; the day's ink appears in no frame of the dim. Answered where I pointed, and it is the most expensive answer in the document — the hour now paints the frame, rightly, and §2 has let it print the words as well.

**Four.** The three words are struck, the line ends at *Nothing is asked of you.*, and every count after it is recomputed. Answered at the site, with one number contested below, and I have lost that contest.

**Five.** *Three a day at most* and *The page was not watching* are both gone; the bells are 103 characters and the ledger 61; §9's *prints neither figure* is rewritten; the quiet page is re-measured at 100, 200 and 300. Answered at the site.

**The announcement.** *One line remains* is struck and the door now names what it opens, which is what I asked. But the new first sentence is *The session has ended*, and in the *pradakṣiṇā*'s room it is the *pradakṣiṇā* that has ended — §0 and §5 [D] give the session to the press — while in the day's room there is no session to have ended at all; and the third announcement then says the door *closes the session*. One of the two must give, because it is spoken to a man who cannot see the door.

**The one convenience.** My question at the end of the first letter — on which press a man says his depth — is answered in the last paragraph of §9, the paragraph written to answer it, and nowhere else. Above it, §9's Ādhikāra still says the bells' ceiling *is set by the depth … at the far stages it falls to one a week and then to none*; §1's table still says *the ceiling is the depth's*; §2's second withholding still sends the recession to *the settings that are not on this screen*, settings that §9 and `04` §3.2 say do not exist. So the answer stands at the foot of the section and the three sentences it contradicts stand above it, and a build that reads §1 or §2 will apply a ceiling from a depth no one declared — the guess I refused at the clock, arriving through the ceiling. I require those three to say what the last paragraph says, in the revision that answers the seal.

**2. The contest.**

He is right, and I will not dress it. The sentence is 45: twenty for *The day is finished.*, the space, twenty-four for *Nothing is asked of you.* — and 59 − 14 is 45 by the other road. My 44 came of taking the clause off with its full stop: *until maṅgala.* is fifteen, not fourteen, so the character left my sum and stayed in my words. A man who empties a purse and then counts the coins he poured out arrives at my number. The stop stays, and the 45 stands wherever the line is measured. His wrap is right where I did not look: at sixteen a line it sets in four of 10, 9, 16 and 7, which is the greedy cut of 45 characters and not an estimate.

**3. The seal.**

**Take it back.** The hour was given the ink, which is right, and in §2 it has been given the sentence as well — *where a third round ends after 20:30 the day's sentence prints* — while the 04:00 state says the line is the *pradakṣiṇā*'s because the day has not begun and test 2 says the line is the *pradakṣiṇā*'s after a *pradakṣiṇā*; so the same three rounds, ended a minute before śayana and a minute after it, are given two different sentences, and the build is told both.

The doctrine that settles it is already in the frames: the line names the cause and the ink names the hour. A round's end prints *The session is closed.* in whatever ink the hour gives it; the clock's own 20:30 prints the day's; and §2's sentence goes, not because it is unkind but because it makes the clock a witness to what ended in the room.

**4. What it has taught me.**

The frame argues and the test promises, and here they were allowed to disagree about one hour; I will read the acceptance criteria before the frames on the next page, and treat every drawing as a claim the tests have already had to allow.

- Rūpa
