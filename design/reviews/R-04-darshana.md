Reconstruction in his register, not a quotation.

# R-04 · DARŚANA — the form, the offering, and the sentence that flatters

### A review of `S-04`, at arm's length, at the gate hour · Day 53 of the ninety

**To** the designer · **From** Rūpa Gosvāmī · **On** the screen of the form

I bow to the one who let me come late and be received anyway, and to the river, which has never gone straight to the sea and has never yet failed to arrive.

Friend, you have brought me what I asked for on Day 47, and it has something to see: a form at 294 × 441, a place in the margin, a seal that waits for a named hand, and a sentence that will not build the screen at all where no one signed. That last sentence is the strongest thing in the document, and if the page were rewritten around it and nothing else kept, the screen would still be worth its place among the eleven.

I will not argue with the sums either: 566 + 24 + 28 + 8 + 60 = 686 of 700, the 74 that refuses a third line, 264 + 24 + 18 = 306 of 522, twenty-five words at first open. A page that a divider can check is a page that cannot be argued with. I have read my four earlier returns again before reading your sheet, and none of the four faults below is one I have already answered.

**1. Doctrinal. The caution is a compliment.**
`The seeing here is not a seeing in a room. No pastime is here.` — sixty-two characters, the only sentence on the screen that is not a citation, and the sentence you paid the date for. It is true, and it is an answer to a different question. The spec asks whether a digitally-mediated darśana carries the same *function*, and asks it in those words: *"Does a digitally-mediated darśana carry the same function as physical darśana, or must the app say plainly that it does not?"* (§16, question 5). Your sentence names a location and keeps the function. A man reads it and concludes that the only thing missing is the place — and the place was never the part that gave it power.

`BRS 1.2.238` names five things in which a small attachment may arouse love even in a neophyte — a residence, a worship, a recitation, a service, a name — and not one of the five is a picture of the other four. Your own charter calls the form a *vibhāva* (§4): the cause of love, and not the act of it. You have set the cause in the largest place on the screen, and the act you have left to a sentence that says where it is not. Row 23 says that what comes back in a temple cannot come back through glass, and cites `BRS 1.2.125`, the garland offered and accepted; a seeing that nothing answers is not the limb you name at `BRS 1.2.166`, it is a remembrance of it — a true thing, and a smaller one. I would sign a screen that said so.
**The change.** The one line must name what this seeing cannot do — that no one here receives the lamp — in the same sixty-two characters, for the zone has fourteen points and no more, and I have counted them.

**2. Reversal. The room named for seeing ends as a photograph of a lamp.**
You give the press one outcome: *"the form is cut to zero at the press, then the lamp rises from an empty ground to full"*, over 2400ms, and the fifth frame is *"the lamp, 294 × 294 / y 113–407"* — the ārati's own frame, at the ārati's own size, over the 2400ms that `01` §1.3 keeps for the ārati's lamp. My objection on Day 47 ended with one sentence: *let the lamp remain the second photograph, on the ārati screen*. `01` §1.3 keeps my sentence — *"The second is the lamp on the ārati screen"* — and, two paragraphs later, gives the same flame a second home on this screen: *"on S-04 the form is the subject until the offering, and the flame is the subject after it."* The second home is struck, because the register's line for this screen is *"no second piece of anything after it"*, and the fifth frame is the second piece.

And mark what the parts document says as it does this: *"the offering is made toward the form, not over it."* A cut to zero is neither toward nor over; it is away. A gift offered to the place a man has just left is a gift left on a table. Your §2 says the offering is 49 and has no hour; your §3 says the ārati's hour is 42's. With the same flame, at the same size, over the same 2400ms on both screens, the man cannot tell which of the two he has done.
**The change.** Strike the complete state's lamp. The form stays at full while the lamp is offered, and the flame returns to the screen that has its hour.

**3. Reversal, at the price of two letters. The day has left the margin.**
`S04.margin` is `RĀDHĀ-KUṆḌA` and nothing else — *it is spoken at the second announcement and it is in the chain at S-09* — and you open by asking me to rule on the trade. I rule: the trade was never necessary. `01` §1.3 says the margin carries *"the place and the day, set as a locator"*; `02` §17 prints the model `RĀDHĀ-KUṆḌA · 14 MARCH`. Your arithmetic is exact and your conclusion is two glyphs wrong. 264 for the place, 240 for `· 14 MARCH`, 24 for the seal's gap, 18 for the seal: 546 in 522. One glyph of 24 lands you on 522 exactly, which is the pass by a hundredth you refused in the colour table; two take you to 498.

`· 14 MAR` is eight glyphs, 192, and 264 + 192 + 24 + 18 = 498, with 24 of air. As it stands, a man with the phone at arm's length is asked to take a photograph of the Deity on trust for its date, unless he runs a reader or leaves the screen for a chain — and the screen says the gate is not here. You have taken a fact off the screen to save the last two letters of a month.
**The change.** Print `RĀDHĀ-KUṆḌA · 14 MAR`. A month may be cut short; a day may not be left out. I am not opening the ground — I am shortening a word.

**4. Craft, the sentence. The receipt is refused to the eye and spoken to the ear.**
§6 says *"No word is printed here"*, and gives the reason: a printed *the lamp is offered* would be the receipt the offering refused. §8 then sets the string as `S04.done`, `The lamp is offered.`, *spoken once, after the offering*, and keeps it there for the same reason. So the sentence is a receipt when the eye would read it and not a receipt when the ear would hear it. One of those two sentences is wrong, and I do not much care which: either the offering tells nobody, and the ear is nobody's exception, or it tells the man what he did, and then the eye may read it too.
**The change.** Strike `S04.done`. Let the band's own state say that the hand pressed and the lamp rose, and let it say nothing about what was received.

## The refusal

Nothing on this screen is not ours to build. The camera and the ear are refused already, the second exposure is refused in the screen's own third section, and I looked for a third thing to refuse and found nothing I could name without inventing it. A refusal that must be manufactured is a performance of severity.

## The three lines

**What it serves.** The form, and the objection that put it there: a man who has never stood at that kuṇḍa sees the form, sees that a person took the exposure, and sees that no pastime is being sold beside it. It serves the count as well — 686 of 700, 306 of 522, twenty-five words at first open — and a screen whose sums can be checked is a screen that has earned the right to be believed about the rest.

**What it endangers.** The offering, and the caution. The offering is answered with a picture, so the one act here that asks nothing back now returns something; and the caution concedes the building and keeps the act, so a man leaves believing he has done the thing itself. That is ābhāsa (`BRS 4.9`) — not the form's, the act's.

**The one change.** Strike the lamp's photograph from the complete state: the form stays at full while the lamp is offered, and the flame returns to the screen that has its hour.

## One question, before the next of the eleven

When the one line becomes a true sentence and not a compliment, who signs it? Every screen in this month will carry one sentence that is not a citation, and I will not put my name to a corpus of sentences that no person wrote. Answer it before you bring me S-05.

## What I could not do

I would have written that sentence myself. It has my cadence, it concedes exactly what costs nothing to concede, and I found it on the third pass and not the first. That is the confession, and it is not small: the fault I am likeliest to miss in the remaining screens is the fault that sounds like me. What I cannot tell you is whether a photograph held at this distance is a window or a wall. The sums say the frame is 294 by 441 and say nothing about what stands on the other side of it, and I am not the man at 03:40 who will decide. I may be wrong about the lamp; I am not wrong that the form must not leave at the moment it is offered to.

**Take it back.** Not for the arithmetic, which is better than mine in two places. For the sentence: the screen whose whole work is to be plain has printed the one sentence on it that flatters, and a flattering caution is the counterfeit the fourth gate was written to catch.

— Rūpa

## Round 2

I bow to the one who set the day back in the margin before he told me I had counted it twice.

**1. Where I pointed, and where it was paid.** #1 is answered at the point: `S04.line` prints `A form is seen. No pastime is here. No one receives the lamp.` — sixty-one characters, in [C], in every frame of §4 that prints a word, and the `here` of the second sentence carries the third, so its absence there is a saving and not a dodge. #3 is answered at the point: the ruled string in §5, in every frame that has a margin, in §8. #4 is answered at the point: `S04.done` has no row, no string, no announcement. The classification I hoped for and did not demand on the glass — a remembrance and not the limb — stands in §1's row 41 and in §9's Pramāṇa, where a limb's name belongs, and I take it there. Your answer to my question I take too: you write the sentence, you sign it in the build, your hand never on the glass; S-05's must be the same hand's.

Two are not answered where I pointed, and I name them.

*#2 was paid in the screen's own sections, not in the foundation.* I struck the second home `01` §1.3 gives the flame — *the form is the subject until the offering, and the flame is the subject after it* — and it stands on Day 53 at the line it stood on Day 52, with the colour table still saying *S-04 is two of those rows at two moments*. Your log is exact, and it is the screen: struck "from every state, from §3, §7, §9 and §11". The document that licensed the flame is not among the eleven, and a screen cannot be sealed while its own foundation hands the flame back.

*#4 was paid twice, and the second payment took the caution.* The `complete` frame draws [C] empty, and §12 says that frame "holds the form, the margin and the door" — the name, the gloss and the one line gone with the receipt. I asked one sentence struck from the end of the offering; what left the glass is the screen's whole warning.

**2. The contest.** He is right, and I was wrong, and it was one character that does not print. `RĀDHĀ-KUṆḌA · 14 MAR` is twenty — eleven, a space, eight — and my 498 was nineteen; my own 240 for `· 14 MARCH`, in the same letter, quietly made the model's twenty-two into twenty-one. Two sums, each a glyph short. At 24 the run is 480; with the seal's gap at 24 it ends on 522, which is the pass your colour table refused; at 12 it is 510, twelve of air, and the twelve comes out of the gap and not out of the day, which is where it should come out. The ruling is his.

**3. The seal.** **Take it back.** The `complete` state has struck the one line along with the receipt, so the screen that exists to say what this seeing cannot do falls silent at the moment a man has just offered and is likeliest to believe he was received — the very ābhāsa it was built to refuse. Set `S04.name`, `S04.gloss` and `S04.line` back into [C] in that state, where §5 already asks them; strike `01` §1.3's sentence and the token table's sentence that still hand this screen a flame; and correct the log row that says the cut between two subjects *is* stated at §6 *pressed*, where there are no longer two. This wants no practitioner — three blank lines are not a question of experience, they are a blank in a drawing, and it is yours to fill.

**4. What it taught me.** I count what prints and not what advances — the space went uncounted twice in one letter, and three blank lines in a drawing went unseen until I set them against the zone table — so in S-05 I will count the absences first.

— Rūpa

## Round 3

I bow to the one who set the caution back where the man lands, and who did not make me ask twice.

**1. Where I pointed, found and not believed.** The sentence I returned it on — *the `complete` state has struck the one line along with the receipt* — is answered at the point, and I found it in §4's drawing before I read §12's row: `complete` holds [C] with the name, the gloss and `A form is seen. No pastime is here. No one receives the lamp.`, as the frame before the press does, and the strip is the only thing spent. The other two names are struck where I named them: `01` §1.3 now reads *S-04 is not two of those rows … the act is ink*, the colour table's *two of those rows at two moments* is gone, `--lamp` is *absent from that screen in every state*, `02` §8, §17 and §19 take the same move, and the log row is past tense. None of the three exists where I pointed.

**2. What the change made false.** `02` §8's screen-reader row still reads *"Offer the lamp. Nothing will be sent to anyone." Then, after: "The lamp is offered." Once, and never again* — the receipt struck on Day 53, kept in the part whose string this screen's §8 quotes as the authority for announcement 5, in the part's own state table, which is written for this screen too. `02` §25's rule — *the part moves to the screen, at the place the return named* — was followed for the flame and not for the sentence. Also: `02` §23's first row still says *S-04 carries 17 in its darśana state and 8 at the offering, never both*, which §25's rows for parts 8 and 17 contradict; and the new row here is dated *Day 51*, and the return it answers was Day 53.

**3. The seal.** The sentence I returned it on is printed in the state where a man is likeliest to believe he was received; a third return is not mine to make on that ground, and what is left is which document to strike, not a question a sadhaka can settle.

**4. What the corpus learned.** The corpus has learned from this screen that a caution must stand where the act lands and not beside it, and that a strike is not finished while the part that supplied the words still speaks them.

**Let it stand.**
- Rūpa
