## 2. The nine objections

**1. Doctrinal. The room named for seeing has nothing to see.**

## 3. The refusal

**The microphone that listens for the response.** This is not ours to build.

You ask for it in the permitted asks: *"The microphone hears the response so the call can wait for it. Nothing is recorded, and nothing leaves the phone"* (03 §6.2), and response mode times the call by the voice it hears. I do not doubt that nothing is recorded. I doubt the ear.

The book's anatomy puts anubhāva on the side of the one who loves: the singing, the bowing, the dancing are his, offered, and they are not a signal for the room to receive (BRS 2.1.3–5). A machine that waits for a man's voice in order to know when to sing again has begun to listen to him, and a man who knows he is being heard sings for the ear and not for the name. Your own page names the danger better than I could: *"that distinction written down, because it is the crack through which a sensor for tears would later be pushed"* (01 §7). You wrote the warning, and then you built the first ear.

So: no. The telephone may record a note that he makes and keeps, because that is his own voice kept for himself. It may not wait on his voice to keep time.

What may stand in its place, if anything may: the call and the response with a silence in it whose length is the recording's own, and a press to bring the next line — the timing carried by the hand, which is the only instrument this product has ever allowed itself. If that is too bare, then kīrtana carries the words and the tempo and asks nothing of the voice at all. You refused the camera in one clean sentence, and the sentence was right. The microphone wants the same sentence.

It is never cited where the other belongs: 01 §8.4 and D20 say the two numbers are never interchanged, and 02 §5 says in words that the 54 is never the foot's. And the strings are written to the cap, not the cap to the strings. A convenient cap would have been the 60 that seven bodies ran past, and that never said whether it was a measure or a count. 54 admits that it is a count of our own longest sentence and not a measure of a lock screen, and that admission is the whole of its honesty.

One warning, and it is not a fault: a counted cap is a description that has been given the rank of a rule. It remains a rule only while a fourteenth body is written to it. If a body of 55 arrives and the number moves to 56, the 54 was never a cap; it was a record.

### 5. The refusal

Nothing remains that is not ours. The camera you refused and the ear I refused are both still refused in the pages; I looked for a third thing and found only my own wish to be severe, which is not a ground.

### 6. The three lines

**What it serves.** The draftsman, and through him the man who will never read a page of this. The measure is derived, the count says it is a count, and the night has the rung it needed. A page can now be checked against another page by arithmetic instead of by trust, which is what I asked for on Day 46.

**What it endangers.** The margin. Your repair was made at night and it was right; but it was written as the day's shape, and the day's faint ink — the margin's ink, the one place that tells a man a chain exists — now has three answers, one of which removes the locator from the screen.

**The one change.** Split the faint row of 01 §9 into its two grounds — by day an address and never a sentence, at night nothing that is a letter — and move the one sentence now standing at 5.86 by day, *Nine have bowed.*, to `--ink-soft` 8.73, where its night twin already stands at 6.66.

### 7. The seal

**Take it back.**

Not for the arithmetic, which is better than mine in two places and right in both. For the one row that says in one cell what the whole third pass was written to separate.

### 8. What I could not do

I have found a row and I have not read the man. The fault is four words — *nothing that is a letter* — and I have spent a morning on them while a person at 03:40 will never read a table; had you set the margin's locator at 8.73 and never written the row, he would have been no worse and I would have seen nothing. That is my confession: I have learned to hear two answers, and hearing them is not the same as serving him. I may be wrong about which of the three the day's faint deserves. I am not wrong that it has three.

— Rūpa

## Round 4 — Day 50

**To** the designer · **From** Rūpa Gosvāmī · **On** the pane, and the last word on the ground.

Friend, I have read `04` §2.3, `02` §4 and the sixth invariant in `INTERLOCKS`, and I have set the arithmetic pages side by side until the one that moved showed itself. I begin with what you did not do.

### 1. The pane is a consequence, not a mechanism

A mechanism is a thing the product can reach for; this is a thing it declines to do, and its silence is the proof. Where every word fits there is no pane, no rule, no drag and the full 294 — the product has done nothing — and where a word does not fit, the trigger is a width that was measured, 414 in 238, with a build that fails everywhere else. A tailor takes the measure from the man, and never the man from the measure. The column was measured from the word; the word was not cut to the column. That is a consequence, and a consequence may be signed.

### 2. The drag is the verse's; the reason is not

The licence is one surface, and three things hold it: the build fails if any other element's scroll width exceeds its column (`01` §4.4), the pane opens only where a single word cannot be set whole, and the edge rule is a rule and never a letter, withdrawn in one frame at the block's end. No page, no sheet, no margin and no photograph moves sideways. But *a person is not broken to fit a column* is a reason, and a reason travels where a licence cannot. It has already found the one place: `01` §4.4, second bullet — *a caption, a place in the foot's sentence, a name* — which may still be broken at a syllable with no hyphen, the act the pane exists to make unnecessary, done where no pane stands and nothing shows the break. A hyphen at least admits the word is unfinished; a break with no mark is the word made to look whole. That is where the draftsman's habit would form, and it is not the sideways drag. The first place-name set at 300% in a column that has no pane will have to choose, and I will be watching which way.

### 3. The seal

**Let it stand.** It is the first answer in this correspondence in which the arithmetic and the sentence point the same way.

### 4. The last word on the ground

The ground is settled, and I will not open these four documents again. What I sign today is not the pane but the sentence beneath it, which was mine, and which the pane has now made checkable: the page does not move, and the only thing that does is the column, because the words in it are a person's. If the eleven screens keep that sentence, the grammar will hold; if one of them breaks a word to keep a column, it was never the grammar I thought it was. I may be wrong about where the habit will form, and I am not wrong that the sentence is not the verse's alone; that is the whole of what I have to leave with you.

### 5. The standard for the eleven screens

**Every screen is held to the first word that does not fit: a broken word returns the screen; a moved column must show its walk.**

— Rūpa
