# R-05 · ŚRAVAṆA — the hearing, the verse, and the end

### A review of `S-05`, at arm's length, in one hour · Day 53 of the ninety

**To** the designer · **From** Rūpa Gosvāmī · **On** one screen, printed at full size, and the verse that is heard once

I bow to the verse that asks nothing, and to the one who first read it aloud to me — whose name, I am ashamed to say, I have never had occasion to put in a margin.

Friend, I have held your screen at arm's length at the hour when the light goes, and I have read it twice, because the first reading was a reading of my own tiredness. Eleven of these are coming before the month is out, and I will not spend the evening on the width of a door while the verse stands in the middle of the page asking to be heard. So I have kept to five things, and each one is a sentence, a number or a string you can put your finger on.

Three things I will not argue with. The next-verse control is refused, and refused in the right place: I will not ask you to put a chevron back into a reader whose whole work is one verse. The verse is at 30/42 and the zone scrolls by 112, and no line is made smaller to make it fit. And the reader has no search, no chapter list and no next chapter; a deep link, a search result and a stored bookmark each open S-09, and the bundle carries no Uttara row until the gate has opened. That is the first trap answered, and answered once.

The second trap you have answered twice, and the second answer is the fault. The provenance sentence has not drifted back into the margin; you have set it at the foot, in `--ink-soft` 8.73, at 18, where the person who asked for 36 will read it at the floor of the product. The margin was the wrong place for it. The floor is the wrong size.

**1. Doctrinal. The chapter's end has been made the verse's end.**

In the Sthāyī you write: *"the verse may be heard again, but the screen does not advance"* (§9). On the next page, of the state after the hearing: *"the act is withdrawn, the chain remains, the door remains, and no next chapter, next verse, summary or sound is offered"* (§10.4). The frame decides between them — *"the act is gone... the door is the only control"* (Frame 2) — and the one sentence that promises a second hearing is a sentence about a control that the first hearing takes away.

The verse is `BRS 1.1.11`, and the third line of its transliteration ends with *kṛṣṇānuśīlanam*. *Anuśīlana* is cultivation; a cultivation is not done once. You printed the word in Devanagari, transliterated it, set it at 24/40, and then built a frame in which the cultivation has one turn. One turn is not a cultivation. The chapter's end is a fact about the text; you have made it a fact about him.

**The change.** Let the act remain after the chapter's end — the same act, the same verse, a second hearing — and let the chapter end because there is no next verse, not because there is no act.

**2. Reversal. The end has no word on it at 300%.**

Of the heading you write: *"At 300% it leaves the screen, because at 320 the act zone's 134 points above the band cannot hold both it and the chain — 84 + 72 = 156"* (§5, [D]). The 84 is the heading at 60/84. But this screen has already set one word a limit: *"the door's word still stops at 36"* (§5, [E]), which is the size it took at 200%. At 40/56 the heading is 56, and 4 + 56 + 72 = 132 in 134 at the 320, and 4 + 56 + 48 = 108 at the 390. It fits, with the two points of air your own table already spends at 200% on the 320.

A person who asked for 300% is the person who most needs to be told that the chapter has ended, and at that size you have told only the ear: *"the ear is told the end in words"* (§6). The eye gets an empty band and a door.

**The change.** The heading stops at 40/56, as the door's word stops at 36, and the end is named at every size.

**3. Craft, the hand. The door's word does not go into the door at 200%.**

*"At 200% the band is 132 and the door's word is 36, one line; the build measures the five letters against the strip's 80 usable points and fails if the word does not fit"* (§5, [E]). Divide it. Five letters at 36 is five times eighteen, which is ninety, and the strip has eighty. `04` §3.1 does the division for you — *At 18pt a character of Spectral averages 9.0pt*, so *the name* is 72 at 18 and 144 at 36 — and the same rule gives *close* 45 and 90. The word is ten points wider than its place, and the build you have written would fail on its own door. `04` §2.3's row already sets the way out's word at *"36, two lines of 36/42"*; your Frame 10, your [E] and your test 7 all say one line.

On Day 50 I set one standard for these eleven: *"the first word that does not fit"* is the screen's test. The first word that does not fit here is the door's own, and it is not broken; it is ten points too wide, which is worse, because a broken word at least admits it is broken and this one admits nothing. The clause that put the word at 36 in an 88 strip is mine — I4 — and I did not divide it when I wrote it. The thumb asks for 88; the word asks for 90.

**The change.** At 200% and 300% the strip takes 104 (x 72–176: 96 usable, 90 of word and 6 of air) and the act 142 (x 176–318, 5.4 of the 48-point targets, its label at 247 inside the 115–275 core); the 88 stands at 100%.

**4. Craft, the sentence. The sheet tells him to press a thing the sheet has taken away.**

Frame 5 is the one overlay, *"the verse is behind the sheet and is whole"*, and its only control is *"close"* at y 700. Its third sentence is `S05.err.recitation`: *"Press Hear it again, and it will try the network once more."* `03` §4.6 makes the third sentence of an error *"what to do next, as an act and not as advice"*, and `03` §4.1 counts it by the same law: *"the third sentence is a pressable act"*. On your sheet the act is not pressable; it is behind the sheet, and the only thing under the finger is the door. A sign that says *press* and points at a wall is worse than no sign.

**The change.** The sheet's band carries the act it names — *Hear it* at 158 in the middle, *close* at 88 at the left — so the third sentence stands over the control it describes.

**5. New. "Nothing follows it" is a promise the app cannot keep.**

§8's script, item 5: *"The chapter ends here. Nothing follows it."* I have not had to raise this before, because no screen before this one spoke. `03` §1.3 refuses a promise about the future in one line — *"a promise about the future is a claim it cannot keep"* — and your own §0 says only that *"the next verse is not here"*, which is true of this screen and not of the calendar. If the next day's 16:00 opens 1.2.1, then something follows it, and the ear was told a thing that is not so.

**The change.** Say what is true of the page: *"The chapter ends here. There is no next verse on this page."*

**The refusal.** Nothing on this screen is not ours to build. The camera and the ear are already refused, and a recording played back is neither; what is here is a page, a voice and a door, and all three are ours.

**What it serves.** A reader that will not turn a page: the verse at 30/42, the chain printed where the thumb is, the gate closed at every path, and an end that gives nothing back but the verse and the door.

**What it endangers.** The hearing, which is made once when the verse's own word means continuance; and the foot sentence, which is present at every size and set at 18 for the person who asked for 36 — the register's trap answered in position and not in size.

**The one change.** Let the act remain after the chapter's end, so that the verse may be heard again and the chapter ends by having no next verse rather than by having no act.

**Take it back.**

Not because it is false. Because the frame is finished and the verse is not, and on this screen that is the wrong way round.

**One question, before the next screen.**

When the chapter has ended and the next day's 16:00 comes, what does the reader open? If it opens the next chapter, then tonight's *the end* was a tide-line, and the water returns by the moon and not by a swipe. If it opens nothing, then 54 has no second day. The screen has not said, and I cannot sign a screen whose end depends on an answer it does not carry.

**What I could not do.** I have divided five letters by eighty and have not asked one man who has heard a verse whether he wanted it again; the arithmetic is mine and the man is not in the room. I have taken the verse's third line as proof of continuance and have not heard the recitation that is meant to carry it, so my judgement of the hearing is a judgement of a sentence about a hearing. Somewhere in these five I have mistaken a number for a meaning, and I will say so plainly when you bring me the place.

— Rūpa

**2 · The contest.**

He is right, and I was wrong. I sent him 104 at both frames and called it a division. At the 390 it is one: 90 of word in 96, 6 of air, 104 + 142 + 48 = 294. At the 320 it is not: the band is the column's 224, my 104 leaves the act 120, and four points of pad at each side leave 112 for a word of 120. The fault I charged him with — a word wider than the strip that holds it — would have been mine, and sitting on his act, which is worse than sitting on his door. His 96 + 128 is the division: 3 + 90 + 3 and 4 + 120 + 4 are the 14 points the band owns, and at 300% the word is exact with no slack. I withdraw the 104 at the 320 and keep it at the 390, where he took it. He is right about the 5.4 as well: that was the act at an 88 band, and at 200% the band is 132, so the area is 8.1.

**3 · The seal.**

**Let it stand.** The verse can be heard again after the chapter's end and the chapter still ends by having no next verse, which was the whole of the first objection; the end is named at 40/56 at 300% and not only in the script; the door's word is divided before it is built; the sheet's third sentence stands over the thing it tells him to press; and the script says only what the page can show. I went to the five places again this morning and found the same sentence in each, not a new one somewhere else.

Two things I settle here, and neither is on the glass. The first is the price at the head of the document. At 360 the space above the band is 192; the rule's 4, the heading's 56, and the chain at 36 in four lines — 192 at 48 leading, 240 at your own — come to 252 and 300. So 360 is short by sixty at the best of it, and the price is the reading zone at 300, or 252 if you keep your 240. I will not give a quarter of the verse's zone to set a citation at the size of the verse; the 18 stays, and it is the right 18.

The second is the row he asks `04` to carry. That row is the act's *name* — the band's own child, two lines of 168 in 192 — and here that is *Hear it* at 60/84, right and unchanged. The heading is the *zone's* name, another element with no row of its own. Let the 56 be a new line in `04`, and let the act's row keep its 60. The glass is true either way; the table would not be if it took the 56.

And the question I left open — what the next day's 16:00 opens — this screen no longer leans on: it says what the page can show and nothing about tomorrow. The question belongs to the clock, and I will put it to S-02 when I read it.
