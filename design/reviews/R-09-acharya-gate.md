# R-09 · ĀCĀRYA & THE GATE — the authority and the gate

### A review of `S-09-acharya-gate.md`, at arm's length, at the gate hour · Day 58 of the ninety

**To** the designer · **From** Rūpa Gosvāmī · **On** the papers of a verse, and the person who is in no frame

I bow to the one who set a place for me at his own table and never asked what I brought to it.

Friend, the light is off the water and the gate hour is nearly spent; I have held your page at arm's length and read it twice, and the second reading found what the first had admired. Three things on it I will not argue with: the act withdrawn in the asked state and not greyed, because a greyed act is an act offered and then taken back; the refusal and the delay made the same screen, because that is the only honest answer an app can give to a question it has no standing to ask; and the chain set at 18/30 in `--ink` in the reading zone, where a man will meet it and not have to hunt for it. Those are decisions, and I have not touched them. What follows is five faults, and I have held to five because an old man with eleven pages before him counts what he can carry.

## 1. Doctrinal. The gate has a term, and the verse you print on it has none.

§1 gives the opener: *"the host of the room he is already in, given to him within forty-eight hours (F5)"*. Frame 7 prints *"You are not in a room yet. A person will write to you within two days."* — 70 characters, and §8 counts them by the same law that counts every other string.

Then §3 refuses *"a date, an estimate, 'about a week', a queue position, a count of requests or of days"*, and §2 gives the reason better than I could: *"it would become false in one move, and the move is a date — 'you will hear within a week' is true on Monday and a lie on the eighth day."* The sentence is `02` §13's, and §13 is the empty floor; `02` §10 is the gate, and it says the gate *"never says pending, processing, almost, or a number of days."* I read that sentence on S-06's floor and did not stop it; it is not less a date for having stood there.

And the verse on the page is `BRS 1.2.240`, by which the depth waits on a man's ripening. A ripening has no term. You have handed the waiting man a schedule for his own śraddhā, and the first work a schedule does is turn a silence into a debt.

**The change.** Strike both terms — *"within forty-eight hours"* from §1, *"within two days"* from Frame 7 and §8 — and let the first-run body say only *"You are not in a room yet."*

## 2. Reversal. The 143 points of air are the invoice for two sentences you cut.

§5: *"The block ends at 423 and the reading zone at 566, and the 143 points between them are the only place on the screen where nothing is said. Nothing is put there."* And §5's last paragraph calls that emptiness the gate's beauty, and the argument is not wrong: a page this exact does not need filling.

But `03` §2.2 had already written the sentence for the word this page refuses. For *Unlock* it gives two: *"This reading is not open yet. The room that opens it has a person in it."* [C] prints the first, 29 characters, and cites `03` §2.2 for it. The sentence it leaves is the one the corpus wrote to say that a gate is a relation and not a key, and it is 42 characters — two lines of the column, 56 points, and the heading becomes three lines, 84.

And [E]: *"The chain's copy is therefore written to eight glyphs"*, so `Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu` is nowhere on the page — while stop 3 of your own announcement speaks it whole: *"in Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu, division one, chapter two, verse two hundred and forty."* The ear is given the book; the eye is given a column. Count it: 423 + 56 + 30 for the name at 18/30 is 509, and 57 points of air stand where 143 stood.

**The change.** Put the second sentence in the heading and the work's name as the chain's second line, and let the page be as full as the truth on it.

## 3. Craft, the eye. The margin at 200% is 546 in 522, and 522 is the number this corpus was returned for.

*"At 200% the locator stays in the band: eleven glyphs at a leading of 40 are 440, and 440 + 24 + 18 + 24 = 506 of 522."* That is four terms. The sum at 100% has five — *"then 24 to the seal's place, 18 for the square, 24 more to the ordinal, and 24 for the ordinal at the foot: 354 of the 522-point zone"* — and the term you have dropped is the ordinal. `01` §4.4 sets the margin's leading at 200% to 40 rather than 48, and the ordinal is a letter in that margin: 506 + 40 = 546. The same page says *"thirteen glyphs fit in a zone of 522"*, which is 520 and nothing else. The empty square has been drawing rent in the band with no tenant, and it is the first thing the sum cannot afford.

**The change.** At 200% the place closes — no person has signed it — together with the 24 that leads to it, and the locator and the ordinal stand at 504 in 522; the day a person signs, the ordinal takes A5's journey to the foot of the block, where it has gone at 300% since Day 48.

## 4. Craft, the eye and the sentence. The ink is quoted from the wrong ground.

[A]: *"the hairline is 1pt in `--ink-faint` 5.86 by day."* But `02` §2's own drawing gives that hairline *"`--ink-faint` 5.12"*, and 5.12 is the true figure: 5.86 is that ink on `--paper`, and the band's ground is `--paper-deep`, 1.14 lighter. The locator above the hairline is the same ink on the same ground, so it is 5.12 too, and 5.12 through her lens is 3.58 — under the 4.10 this page refuses three times, and under the night, which your own row sets at 6.66 *"because a locator is a letter."* This is the one page in the eleven whose whole subject is the address, and the address is the least legible thing on it.

**The change.** Print the hairline at 5.12, and set the locator at `--ink-soft` 8.73 by day, as the night already sets it at the second rung.

## 5. Seal III has a thirteenth path, and no build can shut it.

§9 V: *"Who may receive it is §2's list, and the no-path answer is Seal III's, item by item."* Eight sentences, twelve paths, and every one of them a way a build could be made to leak. I tried to break in, as the register says I do. I did not use a deep link or a QR. I read one line of the opened verse and typed it into a room's thread, and thirty people read it that evening.

Seal III's own sentence includes *"a well-meaning friend's share"*, and the page answers it with *"a link opens the gate on the friend's phone"*, which is the link's answer and not the share's. The share is a man's hand, and no build reads a hand.

**The change.** Name the thirteenth path in §9 V and in test 5, and let Seal III say what is true — the app opens no path — because *any path* is a promise about a man's fingers, and a seal is not a wish.

## 6. The refusal

**The letter that says no.** §1: *"If the person writes a no, it is a letter in the room with his name on it, and that is the right place for it... a man who has been refused should be able to name the man who refused him."*

This is not ours to build. The app carries a question up; what comes down is an opening or nothing, and never a judgement, for a verdict with a name on it is still a verdict. §2 has already said the request is private and *"no sentence anywhere that a man has asked"* — and a letter that says no is that sentence with a signature on it, read by thirty people who now know what he wanted and did not get. What may stand: the host writes what he would write to a man he knows, and the gate answers by opening or by being what it was. §11 has already priced the silence — *"a man may wait at a gate that will never open"* — and I hold you to the price, because a refusal a man can name is a refusal a man can tell.

## 7. The three lines

**What it serves.** The rule itself, printed for the man it keeps out: a chain at reading size in the reading ink, one press, and a silence that is the same silence whether a person has said no or said nothing. It is the only page in the eleven that is not for him, and that is why it is for him.

**What it endangers.** The waiting, twice over: a term that turns śraddhā into a schedule, and a margin that quotes its own ink from a ground it does not stand on, so the address of the verse — the one thing this page is — is the first thing her eye loses.

**The one change.** Strike the two days; the gate has no term, and the page's own auciya has already said so in a better sentence than mine.

## 8. One question, before I sign anything else

There are eleven pages and one hand in all of them, and it is the sādhaka's. §9 V says the one thing that opens it is *"a person writing"*; §2 forbids a list, a count and any sentence that a man has asked; and §5 has no zone for a host and no frame for him. So tell me, before the release and not after: **which of the eleven does the person open, and what is the first thing he reads on it?** If the answer is a twelfth page, say so, and I will read it at this hour and not at a kinder one.

## 9. What I could not do

I have been let in at every gate I ever came to. My brother took me in when I had nothing to bring, and a man at Prayāga gave me ten days and a book, and I have never once stood outside a gate that someone I could not name had to open. So the first-run state is the one page in the eleven I cannot read at arm's length, and I have judged it by arithmetic, which is what a man does when he has no memory of the thing. I may be wrong that two days is a promise. I am not wrong that I have never waited.

**Take it back.**

Not because the gate is a wall — it is the best answer to a wall in the eleven. Because it has promised a date and cut the two sentences that said who is behind it.

- Rūpa

## Round 2

**To** the designer · **From** Rūpa Gosvāmī · **On** the second reading, the frames, and a word that will not fit

Friend, I have read the page again and the frames with it, with a straight edge on the margin, because a page can set its sum right in prose and still draw the sum wrong. ### 2. The contest

Break the word and my own return forbids it; shrink the chain on that one phone and `04` §2.3 refuses a cap in a setting, and a chain set small is the disclaimer the register names; widen the column and `04` refuses that direction too. So I withdraw the clause, and I will not dress the remainder: a chain that says *the verse that keeps it* and leaves the book in four letters is thinner than the page's subject. Redraw frame 6's margin and write in §5 [A] where the square stands when the locator has gone to the foot. Nothing else in the page is false, and I return it for a drawing and not for a doctrine. Number the contest while you are at it: four places send a reader to §13 and the page stops at §12.

**At §8** I say the word he asked for: draw it — the host's one hand, the name first, no count. It is not one of the eleven and it does not hold this page.

### 4. What this screen has taught me

I will read the next screen at its worst size before its best: I measured a column at 100% and asked for a word in it, and a man who has looked at a door from the street has not carried the cot through it.
