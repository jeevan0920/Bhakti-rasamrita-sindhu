# R-08 · ANARTHA — the obstruction

### A review of `S-08`, at arm's length, at the gate hour · Day 56 of the ninety

**To** the designer · **From** Rūpa Gosvāmī · **On** five questions, one address, and a hand that may pass

I bow to Rādhā-kuṇḍa, which teaches better than I do, and to the two who read the Bhāgavata to each other there, because one copy was all they had between them.

Friend, the gate hour is late and my eyes are what they are; I have held your page at arm's length and read it twice, and the second reading forgave less than the first. Three things on it are right and I will not argue with them. The five answers are not kept, and the build fails on a byte — a refusal with a wall behind it. The act is withdrawn in the empty state and not greyed. And the screen whose subject is *anartha* does not print the word, though it is the only one of the eleven with an excuse to. Those three I would keep if I kept nothing else.

## 1. Doctrinal, and it reverses me. The five questions have no pramāṇa a man can reach, and their address has been sent to a room that does not exist.

§1 is candid to the point of indictment: *"they are the designer's sentences for those acts and not the verses' words. That is a claim, and this document owes it an address rather than a resemblance."* Then the debt is paid with a sentence rather than with an address: *"a man cannot check a question from this page. He can check it one room away, and the room is one he walks to."* An address kept in another room is a key kept at the inn. My first gate asks for the book, the verse, the one who translated it and the one who checked it, and says that where a link is missing the thing is not discussed further; here the missing link is not the verse, which you have, nor the limb, which your table numbers, but the hand that checked the sentence. §9 says they are *"mine"* and that *"no verse is quoted and no rendering is offered as a translation"* — honest, and it leaves the chain with two cells empty and with no form in which to leave them empty. Worse, §12's row for my objection 2 reads *"the chain is printed where the chain is"*, which could be true of anything, and then implements it as the audit's page at S-09. My objection said where: the short chain in the one line of the screen that carries the sentence, the full chain only where S-09 already stands. You have kept the full chain, dropped the short one, and made a page that has no document in this repository the condition of this page's correctness — *"the build fails if a question is drawn with no row there."*

**The change.** Let the one line under the heading carry the limb's verse and the one fact the chain has no cell for, that the sentence is ours and not a translation; I will amend `I2` from two forms to three, because a third form is mine to give and not yours to invent.

## 2. Doctrinal. A limb of one day has been made a question of every day.

`S08.q2`, 29 characters, is *"What did you go without today"*, and your table gives it aṅga 9, the fast, at `BRS 1.2.76`. The fast is ekādaśī's. `Upadeśāmṛta 2` puts *niyamāgraha* among the six that destroy bhakti, and a rule is not less a rule for arriving as a question. On the other three hundred and forty days the page asks a man to account for an abstention the book does not ask of him that day, and asks it in the one place where he cannot answer without a small shame. §6 is proud that *"the page reads no calendar"*, and calls not knowing the day *"the only way the fast can be a fact in a form without the form performing it."* But a form that cannot tell ekādaśī from a Tuesday is not sparing the fast a performance; it is asking the fast's question at the wrong tide, every day, and calling the regularity a freedom.

**The change.** Either the page reads the one day — ekādaśī is in `03` §8.1's permitted column, so the word is allowed to it — or aṅga 9 leaves the register's line and the form has four.

## 3. Reversal. My Day-50 standard is certified as kept in the one place you did not look.

On Day 50 I set one test for these eleven: *"a broken word returns the screen."* Your first paragraph asks me to lift it for his word, and argues it from `I6` — *"a second licence would make the first one a habit."* But the pane was never a licence. On the same day I said what it was: *"A mechanism is a thing the product can reach for; this is a thing it declines to do."* You have turned my consequence back into a privilege and spent it like a budget. Then §12 certifies the test: *"no line of ours is broken."* Frame 11 sets the act's verb at *"60/84 in two lines"* in a strip of 158 — one word, set in two lines, above a field of 54 — while on the same page the heading stops at 40/56 and the door's word at 36, as every other label here does. Two labels stop at their large-text size and one grows, and the one that grows is the one that breaks.

**The change.** Let the verb stop where the heading and the door's word already stop, at 40/56, and be set whole; and strike §12's row until the row is true.

## 4. Auciya. The page states its hour twice, with two answers, and the hour it claims is past your own night.

§2's table gives *"20:30, śayana, before rest."* Two paragraphs below, §2 says *"The audit is not in the eight junctions and it is not an act of the clock"*, and *"the only thing that limits it is that the day has other acts."* Two answers to one fact is the single fault this corpus was built to catch, in your own file's words: *"Two answers to one fact is not a difference of opinion"* (INTERLOCKS §1). And the hour it claims is the one your charter has taken away from screens — he reads at eight, and there is no screen after, and the silence is modeled from that hour. The last thing a tired man would do before sleep is count what he took that he did not need.

**The change.** Answer the gate once, in one line: the page has no hour, and is fitting when he comes to it from the day; and let 20:30 leave the table.

## 5. Craft, the hand. The only filled thing on the page is the control that lets him not answer.

§5 [B] takes the field's box away — *"no border, no box, no fill, no placeholder"* — and lets the caret arrive later. But `02` §5 says of this band, *"The band is the only filled object on the screen"*, and here the band is *Answer*, 158 wide, in `--tulasi`. So a man meets a question, a rule, and air, and the one thing coloured and within reach is the thing that takes the empty field and moves on. §5 [B] calls that *"a blank field is an answer and is accepted without a word."* A blank is an answer only if he meant it. A page whose whole subject is his own words has made the way past them the brightest thing on the glass, and five presses will do what the audit was for.

**The change.** Let the field be visible before the first press — the rule at 2pt, the caret standing at the head of the line from the first draw — or withdraw the act until there is a word for it to take.

## 6. The refusal

**The empty floor, and the test behind it. Not ours to build on this page.** §2 says *"The empty state is that refusal drawn (Frame 2), and it is drawn because the page can be reached by a stored path and the resolver is not the only door."* But §9 answers the gate architecturally: *"it is not one of the eight junctions, it has no bell in the thirteen, and the day's own pages do not carry it as an act."* A page that stands nowhere in the day needs no floor; a page that draws one has had to decide at the door whether a practice has begun, and the sentence it decides with is *"This page arrives after the practice, not before it."* That is the gauge I refused on Day 54 when the clock wished to know his stage — *"a handset infers a heart from gaps and hours"* — and I will not take it back because the instrument has moved from the clock to the door. Frame 2 goes, and its sentence with it, and `02` §19 is right that this screen carries no part 13. The register's line stands, and it is kept the only way it can be kept: by the page not being on the day. A page that is absent owes no verdict to the man who cannot find it.

## 7. The three lines

**What it serves.** The refusal of the tally, made structural: five answers and a sentence kept nowhere, so that no week can be held against the last one and no man can be shown his own soul in a chart. That is the one act in this product that costs the product something and not the man.

**What it endangers.** The address and the hand. Five questions stand on a page with no chain, pointing at a room without a document, and the only control that is coloured carries a blank past them; a man could complete this audit five times and never write a word.

**The one change.** Print the address where the question is read: the limb's verse, and the sentence marked as ours, in the one line that already stands under the heading.

## 8. One question, before S-09

The page keeps nothing, so it cannot know it has asked these five before, and on the hundredth evening they will be the same five, in the same order, in the same words. What separates the hundredth asking from a rule he keeps because the page keeps asking him — and if nothing does, what may the page remember that is not a record of the man?

## 9. What I could not do

I have asked you for a third form of the chain, and the two that stand are mine: I signed `I2`, which says no fourth form, and I signed the sentence that sends the full chain to S-09, and then I came to this page wanting a short chain the corpus never wrote. That is mine, and it is half of the first objection. I have also judged five questions about a man's day at arm's length with eyes that are not what they were, and I have never once answered such a question myself — in a form, at night, with nothing kept and no one to hear it. I do not know what a man writes in the sixth place. I have written as though I did, and the hundredth evening is a thing I have never had.

## 10. The seal

**Take it back.**

- Rūpa

## Round 2

*Day 57, the morning after.*

Friend, six objections went out and five came back to the place I had put my finger; the sixth is standing in another man's courtyard. The contest at the end is a good one, and he wins it.

**1. Where each one landed.** My first is where I asked for it: `BRS 1.2.75 · not a translation` sits in the one line under the heading, changes with the question, and the build no longer waits on another page's rows. One clause of §1 still promises more than the page has: the reviewer is named nowhere in this corpus, the word at S-09 is *unnamed*, and the page should print the word that is there. My third is 40/56, whole, 120 of 158, the Day-50 row struck and not retyped; the one break left is the man's own word, declared at the top before I could find it, and I asked for a field that shows itself and not one that edits him. Had the break been in a word of ours, this would be a different letter. My fourth is one hour, given once, and 20:30 out of the table. My fifth is the rule at 2pt and the caret standing from the first draw, which is what I asked for with my own pen; the departure it owes is mine to own and not his to defend. My sixth is Frame 2 gone, the empty state gone, and the adhikāra line kept by absence.

**The one answered elsewhere.** My second. The form has four and the fast is the clock's, and §1, §2, Frame 7 and §12 all say so. But SCREEN-REGISTER's S-08 still reads *five questions* and still carries `5, 6, 9, 10, 15, 19`, and F10 still reads *weekly, five-question*; the *weekly* is not even in his log, and a page that cannot know a week has passed cannot be the weekly audit the spec asks for. The spec is mine to rule on, and I rule it here: F10's act is four questions and it has no week. But §0's *"The register's act has four questions"* is a sentence about a file nobody opened. A promise to pay is not a payment.

**2. The contest.** He is right and I was wrong, and the wrong is one pronoun: *ours*. `03` §1.1 says the app never says *we*, because there is no we; a page that prints *ours* has invented a speaker to hold the sentence. The number holds too: 46 and 56 make 102 in a 90, which is a fourth line and 156 in the 134. My 46 would have bought my wording with the count of four before he wrote, and the count is what a man needs first. The 30 says what the chain cannot say — there is no translation here — and leaves the translator and the reviewer unnamed. He could have printed my sentence and let the count go; he brought me the number instead. I grant the third form as the 30, with the middle dot, and with the reviewer and the translator unnamed because it renders no verse; the foot's two are untouched and no fourth exists anywhere.

**3. The way in.** This is mine and I correct it. I wrote that the page is kept "by the page not being on the day," meaning not on the clock; §2 now says he comes to it from the day and, in the same breath, that it is not on the day's pages as an act and that the app never offers it. If the day carries no word for it, there is no coming. Name the word — a word and not an act — and the page has a way in that is not a bell.

**4. The seal.** *Take it back*, for the one sentence of §0 that says the register's act has four questions: the register's act still has five, and the objection I pointed at the register was answered in this page's prose instead. No sadhaka is wanted for that; a file is. Put the register's line to four, name the way in, and set the reviewer's word to what S-09 prints, and I will read it again gladly.

**5. What I carry to the next page.** I will read the next screen's refusals before its sentences, because here the deletions are the argument and the type is only where the argument was written down.

- Rūpa

## Round 3

*Day 57, the same evening.*

Friend, I did not read the table. I went to the rooms.

**1. What is printed.** The sentence I returned it on is in the file, and I found it: `SCREEN-REGISTER.md` line 26 reads *He answers four questions in his own words and writes one sentence, and nothing he writes is kept*, aṅga `5, 6, 10, 15, 19`; `LAHARI-spec.md` F10 reads four questions, *no week and no bell*. §0's sentence is now a fact about a file a man can open, so it stands. The reviewer's word is *unnamed*, and S-09 prints it — of another verse, which is §4.

**2. The word named and not printed.** §2 says *the day carries one entry that is not an act: the word `the questions`*. I opened the day: S-02's six acts are three āratis, japa, śrīmad-bhāgavatam and before rest; its band is *Begin* and *the name*; §3 refuses *a second screen behind this one*. The string is on no screen but this one, where it is the heading. A word that is the heading of the page it leads to is a door painted on the door. The register says the page is *reached from the day*; the day has no wall for it.

**3. The fast, forwarded.** §1, §6 and §11 hand aṅga 9 to *the clock*. The clock's own festival paragraph says that on ekādaśī it is *the same eight hours, the same six acts, the same door*, and that *a fast announced by a clock is a fast being watched*. The limb is sewn on a sleeve that refuses it. Removal was yours to choose; but a removal that addresses the thing to a page which declines it is not a removal, it is a forwarding address.

**4. The chain's room.** §1 says the full chain *stands at S-09* and the reviewer's cell there is *unnamed*. S-09's chain is the gate's verse: its foot line is *BRS 1.2.240 · reviewer unnamed · translator unnamed*, and it holds no row for `BRS 1.2.75`. The unnamed is the gate's word, not this question's; the short chain is true, and the long address is another verse's.

**5. The frame at the narrow width.** Frame 10 is drawn at the 320 — *260 of 272* — and counts the question at three lines of 60. The longest of the four is 46 characters, and at 36 points fifteen go into 272, so it is four; the rule at 248 and the field's 273 were counted for three. Frame 11, at the same 272, counts four lines of 90 and then gives ten to the line, which is five for that string.

**What it teaches.** The corpus has learned that a page is held by the addresses it leaves and not by the acts it refuses: here the fast is sent to the clock, the word to the day, and the reviewer to S-09, and not one of the three rooms will take them.

**Let it stand** — for the sentence, which is printed; no sadhaka is wanted, three files are.

- Rūpa
