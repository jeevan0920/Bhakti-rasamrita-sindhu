# R-01 · DEHALĪ — the threshold

### A review of `S-01`, at arm's length, at the end of the gate hour · Day 54 of the ninety

**To** the designer · **From** Rūpa Gosvāmī · **On** one screen, printed at full size

I bow to the one who gave me a door and no sign to hang on it, and to the stone of a threshold worn in the middle by people who were not looking at it.

Friend, you brought me one page at the hour when the tide is out in me and the light is going, which is the right hour for a page and the wrong hour for a draftsman. I have held it at arm's length and read it twice: once with the numbers, as your trade reads, and once as the man at the bus stand will read it, without them. What follows is the second reading, corrected where the first one caught me out.

Three things I will not argue with. No account, no greeting, no permission. The verse as the largest thing on the page. A first frame painted and not played. The page is not a shop window; it is too bare for that, and its danger is the other one — a door that goes where its sign does not say. It fails in six places, and the first is the verse itself.

**1. Doctrinal. The one sentence a stranger meets from the book has lost the word that makes it a practice.**

`S01.verse.en` reads: *Love in its highest form asks nothing at all, is covered by neither knowing nor doing, and moves only in the direction of His pleasure.* The verse is BRS 1.1.11, and its verb is *anuśīlanam* — cultivation, the doing of it again and again; its *anyābhilāṣitā-śūnyam* is the emptiness of other desires, not of every claim upon him. Your rendering has three verbs and not one of them is a practice: love asks, is covered, moves. A stranger reading it learns that the highest thing is a state that happens to a man, and then the page tells him, in almost the same words, that nothing is asked of him; he will take the second sentence as the verse's own, because it is the first page and he has nothing to compare it with. That is the counterfeit your fourth gate exists for — not a false verse, a true verse made to say the app's sentence in the book's voice. No one has raised this on this project; it is the one fault here that no arithmetic would have found.

**The change.** Put the cultivation back — *The highest bhakti is the cultivation of Kṛṣṇa's service, in a mood that pleases Him, empty of every other desire and uncovered by knowing or doing* — and let 01 §2.4's specimen take the same sentence, because one verse may not carry two renderings.

**2. Reversal. Three limbs are named and none of them is done on this screen.**

§1 says the verse is 43, "and **Begin** opens it in the reader, where the hearing is done"; the table's third column for 42 and for 63 begins with the word *nothing*. My ruling on H5 was one sentence — *the act must be the screen's own aṅga* — and on this page the reading is finished before the press. The verse is 522 in 522 at 26/38, and a man has all of it without touching anything; `Begin` then opens the same verse at 30/42, where it is 634 in a zone of 522 and scrolls by 112. So the press is not a page-turn, because there is no second page of text; and it is not the door, which is at the left. It is a re-read, and it costs him a scroll to finish what he had already finished. You wrote this fault in your own hand and called it a scruple. It is not a scruple.

**The change.** §1 becomes one line — *no limb; the threshold's authority is Seal VI* — and `Begin` goes, because the reading needs no press and a lintel is not pressed. The page's one control is the door, the register's row for S-01 says what §1 says, and the id `S01.act.label` in 03 §10.3 goes with the press, since a string with no place is the one thing that page will not build.

**3. Reversal. The door names an act and travels to a place.**

Zone [E]: *its word names where it goes: close, the day (I4, A2)*; the spoken stop says *Close. The day.* I4's law is that the word names the destination — *the name* on the clock, *close* on a reading screen, *the day* on the name — and *close* is honest only where there is something to close. On the first frame of the product there is nothing. *Close* is the one word in the corpus that offers to shut what he has just opened, and it takes him instead into the clock: eight arcs, an hour, a next act, the most that has ever been asked of him and the first thing he did not ask for.

**The change.** The threshold's door says *the day*, and *close* stays where it is true.

**4. Craft, the hand. The door's word cannot be set at two hundred per cent, and your own test says it can.**

§6: *The door's word is set at 36 and must be one line in its 80; the test at §10 measures it*; and §10's sixth test passes on *the door's word one line in its 80*. At 18 a character of Spectral is 9.0 points — 04 §3.1 counts *the name* at 72 — so `close` is 45 at 18 and **90 at 36**, in a strip with **80** usable after four points of padding at each side. One line is ten points too wide, and two lines cannot be had, because *close* is one word and a word is not broken. 04 §2.3's two lines of 36/42, 54 and 72, were reasoned from *the name*, which is two words. This word is one.

**The change.** At 200% the strip takes 104 and the act 142, so the word has 96 of measure for its 90; or the word stops at 30 and the page says so in words. Not a test the build must fail.

**5. Craft, the eye. The two seconds are the frame's zones in the frame's order, and the second of them is an ink your sight page fails.**

*The two seconds. Before he touches anything, in the order the eye takes it* — and then the ground, the margin, the verse, the rule, the act's name, the band: [A] to [E] with the ground put first. That is your legend, and it is not an eye. The second item is the margin at `--ink-faint` 5.86, which 04 §2.2 measures at **4.10** and marks *fails* at thirty, forty-five and sixty centimetres — an ink permitted for an address and never for a sentence, and nobody reads a rotated address at arm's length. The eye finds it last, if it finds it at all.

**The change.** Strike the margin from the two seconds, let the heading say what the list is — the order the page is built in — and let the two things a stranger meets be the verse and the one thing he can press.

**6. Craft, the old phone. The frame your legend promises is in neither section it names.**

§4's legend: *The frames are at 390 × 844; the 320 is in §6 and §10.* §6's thirteen states draw no 320 frame, and §10 names the width once, in the seventh test, for the 300% pane. At 320 the column is 224, and at 26 the measure is 17 characters, so the rendering alone is eight lines and 328 points; the block cannot be less than 560 in a zone of 522, and 270 on the 320 × 568 that 04 §2.1's own table carries. *522 in 522, no scroll, the whole verse without touching* is therefore a true sentence about the phone you drew and a false one about the phone in the drawer, which is the phone Seal VII is named for.

**The change.** Draw the 320 at 100% with its block and its scroll, and let the frame's claim be made about the phone it was measured on.

**The refusal.** Nothing on this screen is not ours. The microphone is not here, the camera is not here, the bell is not here, and no third party stands in the room; what is wrong with this page can be mended with a word and a wider strip. I looked for a thing to refuse, and found only my wish to refuse one, and a wish is not a finding.

**What it serves.** The stranger, and the one thing he can be given before he has a name: a verse with its address, on a page that asks nothing of him before it gives. No screen in the corpus is asked to serve Seal VI so exactly, and the register is right to put this one first.

**What it endangers.** The verse. The one sentence a man reads from this book on his first evening has been made to say that love asks nothing, and the page beside it says the same in its own voice; he will believe the second because of the first.

**The one change.** Put the cultivation back into the rendering, and let the page print a definition a man could practise.

**Bring me the person.** The six faults are to be mended, and they are not why I cannot sign. You have answered the stranger with the tenth test and a row in §2, and a sheet is not a man. Bring him to the gate hour — one who has never heard of the book, told nothing, the sound off, the radio off, the phone in his own hand — and let me watch his thumb for the first two seconds and say nothing. If he presses before he has read a line, the verse was furniture, and no word of mine will mend it.

**The question.** Who is the translator, and on what day does his name stand in the line? Until I have that name I cannot sign the next screen either, because the line stands at the foot of every surface that reads, and a rendering I have corrected is still a rendering with nobody behind it — a rope thrown to a man on a bank with no one holding the other end.

**What I could not do.** Five of the six I found by arithmetic, which is the cheapest way to be right and the least useful; the sixth I found because the verse is mine, and I know what a rendering leaves out when a man writes it in a hurry and means well. What I cannot do is see the page as he will see it — in a queue, with the sun behind the glass, in a language he does not read — and I have asked you to bring him because the first two seconds are the one thing no pencil will check. I may be wrong about the door's word; a man may read *close* and hear *leave this*, and be glad to arrive at the day. I am not wrong that he did not ask for eight arcs.

— Rūpa

## Round 2

### The threshold, read again · Day 55

**To** the designer · **From** Rūpa Gosvāmī · **On** `S-01` as revised, and the pages it names

Friend, the page itself is now the one I asked for: a verse a man can practise, one control, nothing asked. Of the six, two are mended whole (4 and 5), one is mended on the page and unlicensed in the law (3), one is drawn at last and disagrees with its own figure (6), and two are mended on the page and only *entered* in the pages they cite (1 and 2). The two that are only entered are the ones that matter to the book.

**1. The six, where I pointed.**

1. **Doctrinal — the screen, yes; the specimen, no.** `S01.verse.en` is my sentence word for word, *the cultivation* and all (§8, frame 1, §5 [B]), and the Pramāṇa gate now argues from the noun and not from three verbs. The other half of that objection was 01 §2.4, and 01 §2.4 still reads *love in its highest form asks nothing* (01:227) and still draws it (01:213); 02 §4 draws the old sentence too (02:179). He has answered it in a clause of §1 and a row of §12 — *the specimen now carries it* — which is the one place a repair can be entered without being made. The specimen's 634 and its 112 below the fold would move with a shorter sentence, and that is a reason to redraw the page, not a reason to leave the sentence.
2. **Reversal — the page, yes; the three rows, no.** §1's aṅga is one line, *Begin* is gone, the band holds one target and no fill, and §6's *complete* is answered with *there is none*. But the register's row for S-01 still reads *may offer one lamp*, with aṅgas 43, 42, 63; 03 §10.3 still carries `S01.act.label`, and not the `S01.act.name` that §5 [D] says it keeps; 02 §19 still gives S-01 `--tulasi` and *Begin*. §1 says the register and 02 §19 *now say* this sentence, and §12 says 03 §10.3 goes with the press. None of the three says it. One thing in the band he did re-draw: §5 [E] gives the empty band as 158, which is the act's old box — with no act and no bell it is 206, as §2 and the legend both say.
3. **Reversal, the door — yes on the page; the law is silent.** Every frame has *the day*, the spoken stop says *the clock, and the next act*, and *close* stays where there is something to close. But I4's law still names three surfaces and not this one (INTERLOCKS §2, and its own minute 4), and 04 §2.3's door row still measures *the name*. A word the page prints and the law does not license is a word the next draftsman deletes. He says in §12 that both were changed; neither was.
4. **Craft, the hand — yes.** 63 in 80 at 18; at 36 it is two lines of 54 in 80 and 84 in the band's 132; §10's sixth test now measures two lines. He is right, and he found the third way, which is better than either I offered: the string and not the strip.
5. **Craft, the eye — yes.** The list is the build; the two seconds are the verse and the one thing he can press; the margin is struck from them in words and in 4.10.
6. **Craft, the old phone — drawn at last, and it disagrees with itself.** Frame 2 exists, and *522 in 522* is now said of the 390 alone, which was the fault. But it draws the Sanskrit in five lines while its own sum counts four (160): the picture is 782 and the sentence 742, and the 220 below the fold follows the sentence. *आनुकूल्येन कृष्णानुशीलनं* is eleven akṣaras, 190 points at 17.3, and fits the 224 column on one line; it is drawn on two. The ▾ at the fold is in the frame and not in the legend's key.

**2. The contest.** There is none, and he is right that there is none: he concedes all six, and the concession is correct. I have been wrong in this house and said so, and I would say so here rather than keep a fault for the pleasure of naming it. But *all six hold* and *all six are done* are two sentences, and the second does not follow from the first. Six repairs to pages that are not his are asserted in §12 and in one clause of §1, and not one of the six is in its page. Whether those pages are his to mend, this page may not say they are mended. I require the claim struck, and I require whoever holds 01 §2.4 to mend the specimen before this screen is sealed — because the specimen is the page every later draftsman copies, and a wrong exemplar travels faster than a right correction.

**3. The seal. Take it back.** One fault, one sentence: this page says 01 §2.4's specimen now carries the corrected sentence, and 01 §2.4 still prints *love in its highest form asks nothing* — so the verse stands twice in this corpus, and the copy that will be copied is the one I struck. The rest of my list is correction, and I expect it before the build; this one is the verse, and I will not set a seal on a book whose first sentence a copyist takes from the wrong page.

**The person.** I asked for a stranger at the first two seconds and I have not withdrawn the ask. He is not what holds the seal this morning, and I will not spend a man on a page that can be settled by opening a file. When the exemplar is mended, bring him: the press is gone, and what he does with a page that now asks nothing of him is the question my first letter could not answer. And the translator's name is still not in the chain, and still not yours to give; *unnamed* is honest, and honesty is not a name.

**4. What this taught me.** This screen has taught me to read the pages a submission cites before the submission itself, because a correction that lives in a log and not in its file is not a correction but a note to the next hand.

- Rūpa
