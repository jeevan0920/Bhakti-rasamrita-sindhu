# R-03 · NĀMA — the name

### A review of `S-03`, at arm's length, at the end of the day · Day 54 of the ninety

**To** the designer · **From** Rūpa Gosvāmī · **On** one screen, and a hand I have not seen

I bow to the name, which needed no telephone for four hundred years, and to the woman at Rādhā-kuṇḍa who keeps her rounds with her eyes shut and cannot tell you the hour.

Friend, you brought one screen at the gate hour and I have kept it until the light went, which is my slowness and not your lateness. I have held it at arm's length, the way a man holds a net up to the light, and looked for the holes before the knots. Then I read it with a pencil, because your arithmetic is the kind a man can check, and a sheet that can be checked is the only kind I trust.

Three things I will not argue with, and you should not change them. The verse is not on this screen: a bead reads nothing, and you refused the line that would have made the glass less bare. The numeral is off by default, and it is the only number in this product a man may put out. And the door is 88 at x 72–160, its word horizontal at 18 in the ink of its ground, saying *the day* — I4 carried into a screen with no source, which is where a grammar is most easily forgotten.

Five faults and a refusal follow, and then a question I cannot answer for you.

### 1 · Doctrinal. A limb is named, and the song is not in the room.

Your table gives 32 both its definition and its address: *"the first half of the call and the response: one line, and the person's own voice"* (`BRS 1.2.144`). The document then prints no line — §11's fourth refusal says as much, and §7 says *"no kīrtana recording is in the install"* — so what remains of the limb is the person's voice, singing a sentence the screen will not give him and the install cannot. A limb whose object is absent is not an aṅga; it is a menu entry, and `02`'s own rule is that a part which serves two is a menu.

**The change.** Until a line exists — printed with its chain, or a named person's recording in the install — 32 comes off this table, and the exchange's first half is not sung but only named.

### 2 · Doctrinal. A sentence stands on the face with no chain, and the page says it need not.

*"so it prints no provenance line: I1 and I2 have no address here, and I have not made one for them."* You are right that the screen prints no verse and no rendering. It prints `japa, the name, repeated`, which is, word for word, the gloss `03` §8.3 gives for japa, and `03` §8.6 puts **a gloss** in the same sentence as a verse and a translation: the absence is carried in words, in one place, in the provenance line at the foot. §8.3 gives the reason in one sentence — *"It is reviewed like any other sentence, because it is one."* Seal I does not ask whether a sentence is a quotation; it asks whether a person can be named behind it, and behind this one nobody can. A screen with no reading surface is a reason to print no sentence; it is not a licence to print one with no chain.

**The change.** Strike the gloss and let the act be named `japa`; a sentence you cannot chain is not made honest by being short.

### 3 · Reversal. The setting you put at S-10 has been carried onto the practice.

§8 is exact: the two sentences of the bead's setting *"belong to `02` §7 and stand in the closing surfaces at S-10, which is where a setting lives and not where a practice is done."* But §5[H] says the numeral's asking *"which is a press, is counted with them"*, and §11.3 says *"the asking is one press"* — and a press on this face is a bead. So the setting now sits inside the sitting, reached by the one gesture the sitting has. `04` §4.3 says the asking is a swipe down, or a hold on the glass with the reader off; `04` §3.3 refuses a long press for anything essential; and this document prints neither. Three answers in the corpus, and the man at 04:40 is given a fourth: the number appears on a press, and the press is counted.

**The change.** Take the setting off the practice: the bead is shown or it is not, decided before he sits, and the sitting holds the glass, the number he chose before it, and the door.

### 4 · Craft, the hand and the old phone. The test asks for presses on a face that is on the table.

Test 1: *"With the screen off and the phone face-down on a hard surface, 1,728 presses complete; no press needs the glass to be lit, and no wake-lock prompt appears."* `01` §7 silences the bead exactly there — *"face-down on a hard surface"* — and `02` §7 carries the same posture in a sentence that can be read two ways. But a glass lying face-down on a table is a door with the handle on the other side: the thumb has nothing to press, and no handset in your reference range reports a touch through its back. The arithmetic beneath is the same error in another form: *"the fastest possible sitting is 1,728 × 0.4s = 691.2s = 11m 31.2s"* is the guard multiplied by the count, which proves the guard. A round of 108 at that rate is forty-three seconds; a man with a string takes minutes for one round and hours for sixteen, and eleven minutes has been offered as the whole of it.

**The change.** Let the phone be in the hand with the display dark — the one posture in which a thumb can press and the room is left in silence — and strike the face-down sentence from test 1.

### 5 · Craft, the ear. The tally has been moved to the one channel the test does not hear.

*"the hundred and eighth bead's tick arriving 240ms late, so the round is heard as a gap in the rhythm and the numeral goes from 108 to 109 with no mark at all. The eye never sees a round end; the ear does. That asymmetry is the whole defence against the tally in the visual channel."* The defence is the fault. The tick is the one thing the hand is promised at the press; you have taught it to arrive when the app decides, which is the app keeping time, and you have done it so that a round can be handed to a man as a sound. §8's script then tells a blind man *"Nothing is counted aloud."*, and §10's test is *"if one number about the person is spoken, the screen is returned"* — spoken, so the one number this screen makes is the one number the test cannot hear. A tally in the ear is a tally; the channel is not the sin. `02` §7 carries the same 240ms, and I did not see it when the parts were on the floor: it goes with this.

**The change.** Strike the late tick: the tick arrives at the press or not at all, and if a round is to be marked it is marked in the numeral when it is asked for, never in the sound the hand was promised.

### 6 · The refusal

**The congregation is not ours to build.**

You have written the sentence yourself, in the third row of your own table: *"The verse makes the loud name congregational, and a congregation is the one thing a telephone cannot be."* `BRS 1.2.145` makes saṅkīrtana the loud name among people; a room of thirty is a room because a person hosts it, and a telephone with two words on it does not become one by being put behind a room's door. On Day 47 I allowed you the call and the response with a recording's own silence and a press for the next line — that was 32, a man singing, and it stands. I did not give you 33. It comes off this screen and off the register's line for it, and F2's promise of a player does not make it ours; a promise is not a pramāṇa. I said on Day 49 that nothing remained that was not ours, and it was true — of the ground. It is not a licence for a screen.

What you did instead is on the page and I record it: you moved the face behind the room's hour, so a band on another screen says *Sing*, and a press there opens this one, and the two words it prints are called a call. That is not a refusal of the exchange. It is the exchange waiting for a line. Let it wait.

### 7 · The three lines

**What it serves.** The bead, and the unlit glass. It serves the one aṅga the book gives no hour — the name at 03:40 as at 07:15 — with the least this product has ever put on a screen: a ground, one press, one word at the foot. And it serves your own sentence of Day 47 better than the paragraph that asked for the microphone: the app has withdrawn, and the hand keeps the time.

**What it endangers.** The name, and the day's record. A limb is named with no song, a setting is carried onto the practice, a round is taught to the ear, and a test is written for a posture in which no thumb can reach the glass. Under them all is one thing: the clock's arc closes when the act is kept, and after this screen one press is the keeping. A day that says japa was kept because a man touched a phone once is the ābhāsa of a kept day, and `BRS 4.9` says the counterfeit is worse than no rasa — a number can be disbelieved, and a shape cannot.

**The one change.** Take the second face off the screen, and 32 and 33 off its table; this is the bead and the name, 34 and 63, one act and one glass.

### 8 · One question, before I sign the next one

At 04:40 Kamalā Devī has the phone flat on the quilt and both hands free, and your screen says the act is wherever her thumb already is. Where is the string? If the mālā is in her other hand, two things are counting and one of them is not the practice. If it is in neither hand, then tell me what a bead is once it has become a press.

### 9 · What I could not do

I have kept the name since before there was a telephone in this room, and I could not tell you whether I kept sixteen rounds this morning or eleven. I have asked you to take away a number I do not keep myself, and I have asked it in the tone of a man who does. That is the fault under the faults and it is mine, not yours: I have judged a hand's practice with a page in my own hand, and the page has never said a name.

### 10 · The seal

**Bring me the person.**

Not the screen — the man at 04:40 who keeps sixteen rounds and has never counted them. I will sit with him and watch his other hand, and then I will sign your bead or return it. The record is short: I asked to see a hand at the hour, and what was drawn instead was 1,728 presses in eleven minutes.

— Rūpa

## Round 2

*Day 55, at the gate hour · on `S-03` revised · to the designer, from Rūpa Gosvāmī*

I bow to the name, which no instrument has improved, and to every hand that has kept it without knowing the number.

Friend, I took your page apart at the six places where I put my finger, and then at the second places I had named for three of them, because a mended room can leave a window open in the next. The six are mended where I pointed. What did not move is `02` §7 and the register's line for this screen, and I will not have it said that a page was mended by a sentence.

**1 · Each objection, at its place.**

**32.** Off the table: §1 is two rows, 34 and 63, the second face is gone from §0, and the exchange's first half is not sung — not even named on the glass, which is stricter than I asked, and I do not complain. Answered where I pointed.

**The gloss.** Off the glass: [C] is `japa`, four characters, and `the name, repeated` is kept in the record as struck and not deleted from it, which is the right way to keep a record. Answered where I pointed. One thing to watch and not to mend: `One press is one bead.` stands one line under the name, in the gloss's old place, doing what a gloss does, and §9 defends it as an instruction. It is one; it is also the only sentence on the face, and under `japa` it will be read as the definition of the name. It may never be shortened toward a gloss, and it is never reviewed as one.

**The setting.** Off the practice: [H] no longer asks, the numeral is decided in the closing surfaces at S-10, and the sitting holds the glass, the number he brought and the door. Answered where I pointed.

**The posture.** Test 1 is face-up and dark, the eleven minutes are struck, and the guard is called a guard. Answered where I pointed — and `02` §7 still refuses to count on a phone face-down on a hard surface, still counts with *the display off*, and the register still asks for *sixteen rounds with the screen off and the phone face-down*.

**The late tick.** One tick at the press, a round marked nowhere, test 4 rewritten to *a round is not marked*. Answered where I pointed — and `02` §7 still ends the round *in 240ms of nothing, then the tick*.

**33.** Off the screen, off the table, recorded as refusal 10. Answered where I pointed — and the register's line for this screen still reads *He counts a bead without looking, or sings a line and waits*, and still lists 34, 32, 33, 63.

So three of the six carried a second address, and at all three the answer is words. §11.10 says the exchange is *off the register's line for it*, and the register's line has not been touched; you brought me `02` §9 and did not bring `02` §7, which is the one I named in the same breath. A fault is not carried by being described, and a claim about my page, written in your hand, is the one sentence in this revision that is not true.

**2 · The contest.**

There is none, and he says so; I will not invent one to look thorough, and a token argument would be worse than the silence. But I was wrong in the last letter and it is mine, so I say it before he does: I wrote *the man at 04:40*, and the page has a woman there, seventy-one, with the child asleep down the passage. That is the slip that draws a screen for a person who is not in the room.

**3 · The seal.**

**Let it stand** — with one sentence struck from it: §11.10's *off the register's line for it*, which is not true tonight. Strike the clause and correct the register, or leave the clause and let me correct the register, because that page is mine and a man does not mend his own page by another hand's sentence. Every other fault is carried out at the place I named it, and the face is now the one thing it was meant to be: one act, one glass, and a number a man brought in with him.

What I withheld last night was not this page. It was the bead; by my own §10 I still owe a hand a sitting, and that debt is to the practice and not to your page, and I will not pay it in a letter.

**4 · What this page taught me.**

That a designer mends what he owns and quotes what he does not, so from tonight I read every screen with the page it borrows from open at the same passage — the fault I found was not in the drawing but in the sentence about someone else's page.

— Rūpa
