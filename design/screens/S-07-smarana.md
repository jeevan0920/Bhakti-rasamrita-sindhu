# S-07 · SMARAṆA — the remembering

*Day 56 of your ninety · Round 2 · unsealed. Where you speak in this document it is the register your charter reconstructs for you, not a transcription. Nothing here is approved until it carries your seal.*

## 0. The act

**He writes one sentence to himself, or speaks one note into the page, and presses Keep, and the page is sealed on this phone.**

The hand does three things and no more. It puts the caret in a field by touching it, which raises the keyboard; it writes, or it does not, or it holds the act's own verb and speaks while the thumb is down and lifts to end the sound; and it dismisses the keyboard, which is the platform's own act, and presses the one filled place on the band. The register's act has two forms, *writes it, or speaks it*, and this screen keeps both, the second as one note and not as a dictation: the microphone is open only while the act is held and closes when the thumb lifts, nothing is transcribed, and the sound is sealed under the page's own key and played back by him alone (§3, §5 [E]). The note has no mark and no place of its own: it is taken by the act, held, and no target outside the act can start it (§10.12). Until the press that keeps it there is no page: the words are in memory, the note is a sound in the phone's own store, and the label says so. After it there is a page, and the page cannot be changed, and the one act it has left is to be let go. The act does not end in a reward, a count, or a word of praise; it ends in a block of text in the reading zone — and, if he spoke one, a sound under the page's key that the words do not carry — and a caret that is no longer there.

**The locator, and why none is printed.** The page belongs to a verse — 03 §11's *the journal's prompt is a verse*, 03 §7.6's *the verse it came from is still here* — and the address is stored with the page, printed in the export and in the after-sentence of a deletion, and printed nowhere else. **The reviewer, and why the chain is absent.** Nothing on this screen quotes, renders or translates, so nothing here has a reviewer and there is no chain to print. **The source of the seal.** It is a fact about the device and not about him. The page is written into its own file, sealed with a 32-byte key generated in the platform's protected store and never written to the app's bundle; the key does not leave the store, and the file is ciphertext at rest. A note, if there is one, is a second file under the same key and is not in the page's text. The ranker of spec §4 has five inputs — `adhikāra`, `sthāyī`, `kāla`, `pātra`, `vitta` — and there is no sixth; the page is not in the corpus index, so it is not in `pātra`, and no module that builds a ranker's input links the page store's reader or the note's. That is one fact with two proofs, a schema and a link, and the build fails if either is broken.

**The one limit I cannot remove.** The seal is against the app and not against a person holding an unlocked phone. On a shared handset the page is as private as the handset's lock, and the app says nothing about that, because a sentence about the lock would be a sentence about the person who shares it.

**The auciya test.** A true thing at the wrong hour is a false thing in effect. The page is fitting at 05:30, at 04:00 and at 03:40 because it asks nothing and gives a blank field; it is not fitting as a page the app opened for him, as a page that arrived with a verse on it, or as a page that greeted him after an absence. So the page never opens itself, it carries no verse, and it says nothing about how long it has been. The state of mind is not a setting and not a reading: the page does not know whether he is tired, angry or grieving, and it does not adjust, which is why the bereaved state and the ordinary night state are the same frame.

**How it is reached.** The page is reached at the verse's end, and it is drawn from the moment it has words or a note. What the reading surface's door does at that end is S-05's sentence and not this page's: I have withdrawn the clause that gave it a new meaning, and this page asserts nothing about it until the two documents are on the table together (§12). This page's own door — the same 88 in the same place with the word *close* — closes to the day, and that is the only close this document rules. A2's promise is untouched: verse, page, day, the name is three presses.

## 9. The five gates

**Pramāṇa — is it true.** Nothing on this screen asserts a doctrine, quotes a verse or renders one, so nothing on it needs a reviewer and the chain is absent by the same fact that removes the margin. What it asserts is a fact about the device, and the fact is testable: the page is ciphertext at rest, its key is in the platform's protected store, the note is a second ciphertext under the same key, the ranker has five inputs and no sixth, and no module that builds a ranker's input links the page store. The address stored with the page is the verse's, printed in the export and in the after-sentence, so a sentence that leaves the phone is never orphaned from the text it came from. And the note is not in the export: the export is one file of plain text with the address line on it, a sound cannot be plain text, and the note never leaves the phone at all — it stays where the key is, under the page, and the only thing that returns it is his own hold.

**Auciya — is it fitting.** It is fitting at 05:30, at 04:00 and at 03:40 because it asks nothing and gives a blank field, and because it is reached through the verse and never through a list of his sentences. It is not fitting as a page the app opened, as a page that carried the day's verse, or as a page that greeted him after an absence. The hour is the verse's hour and not the page's: the page has no hour, no bell, and no place in the day's eight — which is the answer to your question in R-06 §4 for this surface, and not for the room.

**Sthāyī — does it deepen the permanent mood.** It gives one sentence and stops. It does not feed the passing wave with a second page, a suggestion, a count or a *continue*; it does not narrate his practice back to him; it does not celebrate the seal; and it does not turn his own words into a card, a quote or a share. The deepening is in what he writes and in the silence after the seal, and the screen's job is to be out of the way of both. The one thing that could deepen a practice and is refused here is the rereading: the page is reached through its verse, and the app never builds an index of his sentences to browse.

**Ābhāsa — what counterfeit does it risk.** Three. The journal as a diary: a body of text to curate, tag, revisit and compare, which is the corpus the register names, and it is refused by the absence of a list and of a search. The journal as a confessional: the app receiving a confession and returning an absolution, which no machine may do; the page returns nothing, and the crisis line points at a person and not at the app. And the seal as a badge: a page that shows it was sealed, a mark, a lock, a count of pages kept — refused, because a mark on a page is a claim about the person and the page has no mark, only the caret's absence. The note's badge is refused with it: *Speak* is a verb and not a badge, and a page with a sound does not draw a speaker, a wave, a dot or a ring.

**Adhikāra — is this person qualified to receive it.** There is no gate on the person and there is one on the occasion: the page is opened at a verse's end, so a person who has not heard a verse has no page, and the app does not offer him one. The gate is not a stage, not a wait and not a request to a person; it is the verse, which is open to anyone who opens it. The crisis language is not a gate: it is a check on the device, it fires once, it files nothing, and it never changes what the page will do next.
