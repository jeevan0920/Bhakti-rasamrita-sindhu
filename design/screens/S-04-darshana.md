# S-04 · DARŚANA — the viewing

## The thing I am least sure of

You ruled that the trade was never necessary, and it was not. The margin prints `RĀDHĀ-KUṆḌA · 14 MAR` and the one line now says what this seeing cannot do, both at once and neither at the other's expense. The arithmetic that puts the day back is at §5, and the one place where I did not follow yours is at the end, under *Round 2 — the one contest*; the seal has since conceded it. What I am least sure of is the question you left with me: who signs the sentence. I do. It is mine, it carries no seal, and the hand that wrote it is kept with the build and never printed on the glass, because a name over a form is the caption `04` §2.5 refuses. S-05's sentence will be signed the same way.

**To** Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī · **From** the designer · **Day 53 of your ninety** · Round 2 of the protocol in the brief's §6.

**The reviewer.** The frame is used only where a named person signed the exposure, and his signature is the 18 × 18 seal in the margin; the chain at S-09 holds his name, the device, the day and the sentence that nothing beyond crop and exposure was done. Where no named person signed, the screen is not built — not a frame with an absence beside it. The reason is `BRS 4.9`: an exposure with no hand is the counterfeit of the form, and a caution printed under a form would be a warning that the thing itself may be false, which is worse than the empty room. The screen's own sentences are mine and no named person has signed them; they carry no seal, and the empty place in the margin belongs to the frame and not to them. **The margin, and why the day is in it.** `02` §17 asks the margin for the place and the day, and the model it now prints is `RĀDHĀ-KUṆḌA · 14 MAR`: twenty glyphs at a leading of 24, 20 × 24 = 480. The full month, `RĀDHĀ-KUṆḌA · 14 MARCH`, is twenty-two and 528 against a zone of 522, the model that was measured and refused (`02` §2, `01` §3.2). A month may be cut short and a day may not be left out, so the run is `RĀDHĀ-KUṆḌA · 14 MAR`: twenty glyphs, 480 points, then the seal's gap and its square. The gap is 12 and not 24, because 480 + 24 + 18 is 522 exactly and a locator that ends on the last point of its zone is the pass by a hundredth this sheet refused in the colour table; at 12 the run is 480 + 12 + 18 = **510 of 522**, twelve of air. Why the gap pays and the string does not is at the end, under *Round 2 — the one contest*: `· 14 MAR` is eight glyphs but the run from `RĀDHĀ-KUṆḌA` to `MAR` is twenty. At 320 the zone is the same 522 and the run the same 510. At 200% the letters are 36 on a 40 leading and 20 × 40 = **800 in 522**, 278 past the rule, so the locator leaves the band for the foot of the reading surface, set horizontally in two lines — 20 × 18 = 360 does not go into 294 either — and the seal keeps the band at 18 × 18, because a seal does not scale with type. At 300% the letters leave for the same reason and by a wider margin.

**The one line, and where it stands.** It is the act's one line and not a caption, so it stands in the act zone: 566 + 24 + 28 + 8 = 626, then two lines of 30, and 686 of 700. At 320 the column is 224 and 61 characters become three lines of 24, which is 90, and 24 + 56 + 8 + 90 = 178 does not go into 134 — so on the 320 frame the one line stands at the foot of the reading surface instead, where the frame ends at 84 + 336 = 420 and leaves 146. The same thing happens at 200% and at 300%, where the line is four and six lines. One rule covers all of them: **the act zone holds a name and two lines; anything longer stands at the foot of the reading surface, which scrolls.** Nothing is shortened and nothing is set smaller. At 320 the frame also keeps the top it has at 390, 84, rather than centring in the zone: the eye finds the form at the same height on both phones, and the 146 points that leaves below it are what the one line needs there.

## 6. States

**first-run.** Drawn at 4.b. The screen at first open is the screen: no tour, no instruction, no welcome, and the same one line. The only difference is the seal, which arrives over `--fill` 480ms when the screen is built and is present at once under reduced motion. If a stranger cannot find the offering from this frame alone, the frame is wrong and not the stranger (Seal VI).

**empty.** Drawn at 4.c. The frame is not on this phone — the build carries it at 0.21MB of 20.21, and if it is missing the screen does not invent one. With no provenance there is no margin and no band: the column becomes 342 at 390 and 272 at 320, and the floor says one sentence at x 72. The one act is the name, because an offering with no form is addressed to nothing, and `04` §6.1 gives the name no hour and no object.

## Round 2 — the one contest

He writes: *"`· 14 MAR` is eight glyphs, 192, and 264 + 192 + 24 + 18 = 498, with 24 of air."* The eight is right for `· 14 MAR` — dot, space, one, four, space, M, A, R. The model he cites proves the counting rule: the full month, `RĀDHĀ-KUṆḌA · 14 MARCH`, is twenty-two glyphs, 22 × 24 = 528, and 11 + 11 counts that space. It is the model that was measured and refused, and `02` §17 now prints the twenty-glyph run. So at 24 to the glyph the run is 20 × 24 = **480**, and 480 + 24 + 18 = **522 exactly** — the point-for-point pass by a hundredth that this sheet refused in the colour table. His 498 has 24 of air; his own string has none.

So I have taken the twelve from the seal's gap and not from the day: 480 + 12 + 18 = **510 of 522**, twelve of air, and the locator does not touch the rule. The day is not in question.
