**The thing I am least sure of**

What I will not do is announce a session that has ended: at 20:30 there is no session to have ended, and after a *pradakṣiṇā*'s third round the line has already said it. The page behind the door stands, because it asks nothing, one door leaves it, and the bells, the ledger and the offers have no other address.

# S-10 · THE END & THE QUIET — the close

**To** Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī, founder, owner, and sole approver
**From** the designer
**Day 56 of your ninety.** Submission ten of eleven. One surface, printed at full size.

## 0. The act

**He closes, and the screen dims to one line.**

The hand does one thing: it presses the door at the band's left end, and the app's closed state is shown. There is no act on this screen, because the act and the way out are the same act, and a band that offered a second verb would be offering him something to do at the one moment the product has nothing to ask.

The door's word is *close*, and it says what it does. What it does is not a departure to another part of the app but the end of the working: the app's own closed state is the quiet page, and that page's door is *the day*, because an app that has closed is opened by the day and by nothing else.

Two closings reach this screen, and they are not the same hour. The first is the *pradakṣiṇā*: three rounds, and it ends. The second is the day: at 20:30 śayana closes and the day is finished whether or not he kept anything in it — the hour is the cause, and there is no last act it needs. Both dim to one line. The line is not the same.

**Why nothing comes back.** The book defines the act as *anyābhilāṣitā-śūnyam*, devoid of any motive other than His pleasure (`BRS 1.1.11`), and a screen that returns a summary, a mark or an invitation has put a motive into the act and called it a kindness. Your sentence at the first review is the same sentence in the register of a man who has watched people: *a man applauded for his prayer has been paid for it, and payment is the beginning of a wage*. So the close gives back one sentence, and the sentence is not a payment: it names a state and not an act — *the session is closed*, *the day is finished* — and a state is not a receipt. The day's is true whether or not he kept anything in it; the *pradakṣiṇā*'s is the state the screen is in at the third round's end, and it counts neither the rounds nor him.

## 1. The aṅga and the source

**No aṅga, and the register's *none* is right.** This screen does not perform one of the sixty-four; it exists so that the others may end. A limb is something a man does toward the Lord, and closing is not a limb — it is the ground the limbs are laid down on. To hang a limb here would make the end into a practice, which is the counterfeit the fourth gate exists to catch. So §1 names an authority instead, and it is one verse and two sections of your specification:

| The authority | Where it says so | What it fixes here |
|---|---|---|
| the six destroyers | `Upadeśāmṛta 2` — `atyāhāraḥ prayāsaś ca prajalpo niyamāgrahaḥ / jana-saṅgaś ca laulyaṃ ca ṣaḍbhir bhaktir vinaśyati` | the six shapes this screen refuses, one to a row, at §3 |
| bounded sessions | spec §4: every feed has a terminal state; a *pradakṣiṇā* is three rounds, and then the screen says so | the close exists, and it is designed before any entrance (Seal V) |
| the bells and the silence | spec §9: three a day at most, never between 21:00 and 04:00, never on ekādaśī night; silence for the advanced | the arming rule and the night window on the quiet page, and the absence of any switch; the page prints no ceiling (R-10.5) |

**The locator is `Upadeśāmṛta 2`, and the screen does not print it.** This is the one screen whose authority is a verse it refuses to show. The verse is two pādas of Sanskrit, and a rendering if it were ever printed, and the close may not offer a final verse — `02` §12 refuses it by name — so the verse stands in this document and nowhere on the glass. There is therefore no margin here: the margin carries locators, and a locator with nothing to locate is an empty eighth of a cheap phone (H4, `01` §3). The absence is not a gap in the chain; it is the one place in the product where the chain is silent because the screen teaches nothing.

**The reviewer.** Nothing on this screen is quoted, so nothing on it needs a signature, and no seal's place is drawn. The two terminal sentences are the copy `02` §12 carries: *The session is closed.* for the *pradakṣiṇā*'s close, and *The day is finished. Nothing is asked of you.* for the day's. They are the designer's words for this product's end and not a translation of anything. The screen-reader says *rendering* nowhere, because there is no rendering on it.

**The one number, and why it is a word.** The screen carries no numeral at all. The hours of the night window are *nine* and *four*: words, because `03` §4.4 makes a number that will fit in a word a word, and a figure on this screen would be the first thing on it that could be counted. No ceiling is printed, and the next act is no longer named, so the hour that stood here has gone with the sentence that carried it. The build fails on a digit.

1. **A screen nothing has ended.** The dim is caused by two things and never by a press: a completion, and the close of śayana at 20:30. 2. **A person at bhāva and prema, for whom the whole app recedes and not merely this screen** (R7). **What the close knows, and the one thing it does not.** It knows two facts: which closing he is in — a *pradakṣiṇā*'s third round, or the hour of śayana — and whether an offer is live on the quiet page. The two terminal sentences are the same on the day he kept every hour and on the day he kept none, because both are true at the hour that prints them: *the session is closed* is the state the app is in when the third round ends, and *the day is finished* is a fact about the clock, which is why the empty day gets it. Neither is a fact about him, and the screen has no way to make it one.

## 9. The five gates

**Pramāṇa — is it true.** The screen prints no sentence of the book, so its truth is a truth about the app, and every sentence on it is checkable against a mechanism. *A bell is armed at one ārati and rings only for it* is `01` §7's arming rule; *none between nine at night and four in the morning* is spec §9's window, and the build fails if a bell is scheduled inside it; *no number is kept of the day* is R9 and `01` §2.3's rule that no figure counts him, and *nothing here says what you did* is its second half, because there is no record on the page for a sentence to be right or wrong about; *the session is closed* and *the day is finished* are one state of the app and one fact of the clock, each of them a line of code. No sentence on the page now states a ceiling: the maximum of three is the build's and not the page's, and a maximum spoken at the close would be a rule where the hour asks for nothing (R-10.5). The screen's own authority is `Upadeśāmṛta 2`, and the screen does not print it, so no reader can check the authority from the glass — which is why this document carries the locator and why the absence is stated at §1 and not hidden.

**Auciya — is it fitting.** To this hour, this hand, this state. At 20:30 it is one sentence and a door for a man who is going to sleep; at 05:10 after three rounds it is the *pradakṣiṇā*'s sentence and the same door for a man who is going to work; at 03:40 it is the night ground, the line at 12.48 and no word that asks anything of a woman whose hands are shaking. What is not fitting and is therefore absent: a summary at 20:30, which would be the day's account demanded at the hour of rest; a verse at 04:00, which would be the app choosing a text for a person who has not asked; a greeting at any hour; and a question at the close, because the app asks nothing and a question is a demand wearing a curve.

**Sthāyī — does it deepen the permanent mood.** The close has no rasa of its own and it must not imitate one. Its danger is the passing wave in both directions: the relief of finishing, which is a small pride, and the flatness of a gap, which is a small guilt. Its service is to end the session without either, and the test is the corpus's own: remove the sentence and ask what is lost. What is lost is the one thing a person carries out of the room; remove the door and the screen is a wall. Everything else on it — the ledger, the bells, the offers — is there so that a question about the app does not have to be asked of a person at the end of his practice.

**Ābhāsa — what counterfeit does it risk.** Three, and each is named before it is refused. The first is the close as *care*: a summary that says *this is what you did* in the voice of someone who was watching, which is the counterfeit of company and the exact shape of `BRS 4.9`'s uparasa, a feeling with the shape of a feeling and none of its substance. The second is the ledger as a *plaque*: an app that prints its own refusals has begun to be pleased with them, and a list of virtues on the last screen is a shop sign hung where a lamp should be. The answer is that the ledger prints two *limits* and not one refusal of a temptation, and that a limit can be tested by a build while a virtue cannot. The third is the quiet as a *mood*: a page that makes silence beautiful, which would be an ornament on the one thing that must not be ornamented. The answer is that the quiet page is a page of facts about a mechanism and has no adjective in it.

**Adhikāra — is this person qualified to receive it.** No one is withheld from the close, and the close withholds nothing: it shows no verse, no pastime and no depth, so it cannot be the deep link that makes S-09 decorative. The one adhikāra question the screen answers is R7's: at bhāva and prema the whole app must recede, and this screen cannot show that recession because showing it would require knowing the depth, which every surface that knows a depth has guessed (S-02 §2). So the close is one face for every man, and the recession is carried by the offers that live on this surface, each made once — the room's quiet, the bead's numeral, the line — and by nothing that announces itself. This screen prints no ceiling at all, because a ceiling that announced itself would be a stage on the glass: the bell is the hand's to arm (`01` §7), so the far stages are a week in which he arms one, or a week in which he arms none, and not a mode the app applies.

**The press he asked for, and why there is none on this page.** His question at R-10 §3 is the right one: a ceiling the app applies from a depth the man has not declared is a guess, and the guess has been refused at the clock. On this page there is no press that asks his depth, and that is the answer and not an omission. A page that asked would have to name a stage, and a man naming his own stage keeps a score of himself — the thing R9 forbids the app and the thing the close must not teach — and the one slot this page has is offered once and holds a limit, not a rank. The bell is armed per ārati and never switched on for ever (`01` §7), so the far stages are not a mode the app applies but a hand that stops arming: a man at bhāva hears one bell a week because he arms one a week, and at prema none because he arms none. What the app enforces without knowing him is what is true of every man — the night window, and a maximum of three in a day — and both are the build's and not the page's. If he rules that the ceiling must be applied by the app and not by the hand, then a declaration has to exist, and the only press S-10 can offer without a settings list is this page's one slot, asked once, phrased as a limit; I would bring him that copy before I drew it, because a switch that asked a man his depth would be the guess arriving with a label on it.
