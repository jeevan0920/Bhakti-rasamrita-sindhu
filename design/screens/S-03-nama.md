## 0. The act

He counts a bead without looking.

One face, one press. The press is one bead, and the glass shows nothing it was not asked to show. The hand keeps the time, because the telephone is not permitted to keep it. Nothing is sung here: the call and the response left this screen at objection 6, and 32 and 33 are off its table at objections 1 and 6.

But a bead has to begin, and the beginning is the one thing on this screen that is not the bead. So the screen opens on a threshold: a rule, the act's name, one line, and the band. The band's press begins the sitting and is itself the first bead — one press, not two — and the chrome dissolves behind it and does not come back. What that costs: a top half that holds nothing, which is the only emptiness in this document that is not a failure, and a hand that must find the band once and may then forget it, because after the first press the target is the whole glass.

**Two doors, and neither is a control on this screen.** The bead's door is the day: the clock's act at prātaḥ opens it, and the clock's own door, whose word is *the name* (I4), opens it too.
