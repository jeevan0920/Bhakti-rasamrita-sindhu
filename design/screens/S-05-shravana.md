## 0. The act

**He hears one verse, and the chapter ends.**

The hand does one thing: it presses *Hear it* at the foot, and the verse is read to him. The chapter's end is not a place he navigates to; it is the state the reader arrives in when the last verse has been heard. He does not turn a page, because there is no page to turn: the reader is one verse, and the next verse is not here.

**The act does not end with the chapter.** After the last verse the heading says *the end* and the band still says *Hear it*, and a second press hears the same verse again. The chapter ends because there is no next verse, not because there is no act: *anuśīlana* is cultivation, and a screen on which the verse could be heard only once had made the chapter's end into the verse's end (R-05, objection 1). What the screen does not do is advance — no next verse, no next chapter, no summary, no sound.
