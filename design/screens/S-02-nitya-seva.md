## 0. The act

He sees the next act of the day and opens it. One thumb lands on a 158 × 88 strip at the foot of the screen, the strip says *Begin*, and the act's own surface opens; the only other target is the door at the band's left end, whose word is *the name*, because the name is the one act the book gives no hour.

The clock itself does nothing else. It does not count, it does not remind, it does not greet, and it does not move unless the hour turns or he presses.

## 1. The aṅga and the source

**No aṅga, and the day for its authority.** The clock has no limb of its own, and the six it was given in round 1 are struck: circumambulation is a walk and not a ring, and the special service of Kārtika is a service and not an hour that stays where it was. The book's sixty-four are acts of a man, not properties of a surface (`BRS 1.2.74–95`), and a limb is not a label sewn on the sleeve — the glossary says it must belong to a body. The clock opens the acts; it does not perform them. So §1 says of this screen what the register says of S-10: **no aṅga**, and its authority is the day.

The register's row is corrected to this, in the register's own columns:

| # | Name | Gloss | Division | The act, in one sentence | Aṅga | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| S-02 | **Nitya-sevā** | the day clock | Pūrva | He sees the next act of the day and opens it | *none* — the authority is the day | the day: 01 §5 (D13); `BRS 1.2.87`; spec F1 |

**The acts it opens are doors, and the limb is behind the door.** The three āratis are 42, offering ārati at the different times (`BRS 1.2.87`), and are answered on the ārati's own surface; *japa* and the door *the name* lead to S-03, whose limbs the register gives as 34, 32, 33 and 63; *śrīmad-bhāgavatam* leads to S-05, whose limbs are 43, 54 and 61; and *before rest* leads to the day's close, which the register gives no limb. Not one of these is a property of this glass, and no arc is the performance of a limb. The six aṅgas this screen was given in round 1 still stand in `LAHARI-spec.md` F1's header; the row above is the correction for that header as for this document, and the register's is the one that decides.

**The reviewer, and why this screen prints no chain.** The clock prints no sentence of the book. It prints a name and an hour: *rāja-ārati*, *12:00*. The provenance line of I2 has no work here because there is no quoted sentence to source, and the margin is absent by your ruling (H4, 04 A10). So this document is the only place the clock's authority can stand, and it stands in one sentence: **the acts are the book's, and the day's hours are ours.** `BRS 1.2.87` gives ārati at different times and does not give a schedule; the eight junction hours in 01 §5 are the app's division of the day, ruled by you and recorded as D13. The screen must never imply otherwise, which is why no string on it says *the text says* or names a verse.

**The one repair I am making to a spoken line.** 04 §4.1's ring announcement and 02 §1's sentence both count *eight acts*. 01 §5's own table gives six acts and two hours with nothing (nirmalya at 05:45, niśā at 00:00), and a fill in either of those two hours would break a page you have already sealed — 01 §9.2 requires niśā's arc to be empty at 04:00, so a name taken at 03:00 cannot fill it. The count is therefore six, and the script at §8 now names the two silences instead of counting six against eight (objection 4); I have left 02 §1's analytic sentence as it stands because the meaning it fixes — the fill is acts and not minutes — is right and only its numeral is the table's. It is a repair of one clause, not a contest, and it is logged in §12.

**Who must not receive it.** Three, and the third is the one I would defend hardest.

1. **A person who has not yet crossed the threshold.** S-01 Dehalī is the first screen a stranger sees; the clock is the second. A clock shown first is a schedule shown to a man who has not asked for one.
2. **A person in a room full of people.** The clock is silent in every state; there is no bell on it and no haptic, so what must not be there is the light. In public mode the ground is the ground and nothing brightens: the ring's fill is a line and never a glow, which is why `--fill` is a stroke offset and not a luminance.
3. **A person who has declared a depth, and no one the clock has guessed.** R7 says the app recedes at bhāva and prema, and the register says the clock must show *fewer* acts there, not more. But to show fewer it must know the depth, and a handset that infers a heart from gaps and hours has built a gauge — which is your refusal, and it is kept. So the depth is not read here: it is a declaration the man makes once, in a place that is not this screen, and the clock obeys the setting and never a guess. When he has made it, the ring still holds the day's hours, the act zone's name is *japa*, the hour is absent — because the name has no hour — and the one line is withdrawn. Nothing on this screen says why, because a mode would be the app announcing its own tact (§7.4). Until he declares it, the clock has one face for every man.

**What the clock does not know, and the one bit it does.** It does not know his stage, because no surface displays one and none is told to it; a depth it showed would be a depth it guessed, and a handset that infers a heart from gaps and hours has built a gauge (your refusal, and §2.3 above). It does not know whether he was away nine hours or nine days, because a day's painting is a day's painting and there is nothing in it that could tell the difference. It keeps one bit and only one: whether yesterday's painting carried a mark, which is what [B] spends and all it spends. It does not know which act that mark was, or how many mornings ago the last one was, or whether he is well. And it does not know whether the act it names was kept yesterday, or ever: the bit it keeps is about the day, never about the act. That ignorance is not a limitation to be repaired with a record: it is the only reason the screen can be met at 03:40 without an apology in it.

**The hour's state of mind.** The clock is read by a person who is not yet awake, or no longer interested, or holding a child. It is read at arm's length with one eye. It is never read for pleasure, and it must never be worth reading twice.

**[A] The ring.** *Purpose:* to hold the day as a vessel that fills only when something is poured into it. *In it:* eight ticks, the arcs of the acts that have closed, the two silences at 0°–45° and 90°–135°, the next junction's glyph, the level hairline. No numeral, no name, no percentage, no ordinal, no second glyph. *Tokens:* 246 across, r=122, centre (195, 305), so x 73–317 and y 183–427; circumference 766.5, eight arcs of 95.8 and 45° each, 766.4 together; fill 2 points `--tulasi` 6.71 day, `--tulasi-night` 5.83 night; ticks 1 point × 6, `--ink-faint` 5.86 day, `--moon-faint` 4.94 night; glyph 18 points at r=104, `--ink` day, `--moon` night; hairline 1 point, `--ink-faint` / `--moon-faint`; silence 1 point, `--ink-faint` day and `--moon-faint` night, at the tick's rung and in no state thicker; `--fill` 480ms but never used on this screen (§7). *Behaviour:* an arc closes when the act closes on the act's own surface, and at no other moment; a junction is missed when its arc closes and not when its hour passes, because the arc is the act's own span and a man who opened the ārati at 04:19 may close it at 04:40. The ring is painted when the screen opens and is not repainted while it is open, except at a junction, where the whole painting is replaced in one cut — no sweep, no creep, no second hand. The hairline is drawn only inside an arc that carries an act: at 09:20 it stands at 135 + 45 × 125 ÷ 285 = 154.7°, which is 42.0 points into prātaḥ's arc; at 23:50 it stands at 315 + 45 × 200 ÷ 210 = 357.9°, 91.2 points into śayana's and 4.6 points short of the top. In the two arcs that carry no act — niśā's and nirmalya's — there is no hairline, because there is no act's span to divide. *States:* day and night; bare of every act at 04:00, partial, full; the two silences are present in all of them, because they are the temple's and not his; a missed junction's tick is not drawn — its tint is the surface's own, `--paper-deep` 1.14 by day and `--kunda`, the one night plane, at night, which is why the contest at the end of this document asks you to keep it so; at 200% the glyph grows and the ticks thicken to 2, at 300% to 3, and the silences stay at 1 point in every state, because at 3 they would be heavier than the 2-point act they are not; the ring's geometry does not scale by one point (A6); on a cracked screen the ring is above the crack and is untouched. At 320 the ring does not shrink: 246 across, centred at 160, so x 37–283, with 37 points of air at each edge and 13 inside each end of the 272-point column.

**Why this is a vessel and not a scoreboard, in four mechanical facts.** One: there are eight arcs and six acts, and the two arcs that carry none cannot be filled — niśā's, because 01 §9.2 requires it empty at 04:00, and nirmalya's, because nothing is offered in it. So the fullest possible day is 574.8 points of arc with 191.6 points carrying no fill at 0°–45° and 90°–135°, and the ring cannot close; a thing that cannot be completed cannot be a score. Two: the two arcs that carry no act are always the same two, at the same two angles, and they are drawn as silences from the first frame, so nothing on the ring is an unexplained absence and the two hours that carry no act can never be read as two failures. Three: the fill is binary — an arc is 2 points of one colour or it is nothing, with no partial fill, no gradient and no sweep, so no arc can be 60 per cent done and nothing on the ring can be read as progress toward a quota. Four: the ring is emptied at niśā, so there is no history to compare and no ninth day to see. And the round-1 third fact — *a missed junction removes its tick, so the denominator moves* — is struck, because you are right that a moving denominator is a count and not a cure for one; what is drawn now is not a defence but a shape.

## 6. States

**First-run.** The screen is identical to any bare day, because a clock has no first-run state: eight ticks, the two silences, the next act, the door. 2. *The ring.* "The ring. Six acts, and two silences: the hour after midnight, and the hour after the morning offering. The arcs that have closed are filled; an hour that was missed is left open and is not counted. No number is spoken here."

## 9. The five gates

**Pramāṇa — is it true.** The acts it opens are the book's: ārati at different times, `BRS 1.2.87`, inside the sixty-four of `BRS 1.2.74–95`, and the three āratis, japa, the hearing and the close are each answered on a surface that carries its own limb. The clock has none, and what it has instead is an authority: the day, its hours ruled at 01 §5 and recorded as D13, and the screen never presents them as the book's, because it prints no sentence of the book at all. Nothing on this screen is a rendering, a translation or a purport, so nothing on it needs a reviewer; the one place the chain could stand — the foot of a reading surface — is absent, and the clock is the one surface with no margin at all (H4). What the screen does assert is a fact about the device: an arc is filled only when an act closed on the act's own surface, and a build that fills an arc from a tap, a timer or a count has made the ring a claim about a heart.

**Auciya — is it fitting.** To this person, this place, this hour. At 04:00 the screen is a dark ground, eight faint ticks, the two silences and one word — and no third line, because a man who has just opened his eyes is not owed a page. At 14:20 at a stand it is ten words and two targets, because a man with the engine off has one hand and no patience. At 03:40 it is a bare ring and no countdown, because the hour itself is the wound. And where a man has declared a depth for himself, in a place that is not this screen, it offers the name and no junction act, because at that depth the app's part is to recede and not to schedule; it never reads the depth from gaps and hours, and until he declares it there is one face for every man (your refusal). What is not fitting, and is therefore absent: a verse at 04:00, a greeting, a question, a number, and the day's full shape at the hour when it is only half kept.

**Sthāyī — does it deepen the permanent mood.** The clock has no rasa of its own; it is the hour at which the acts that carry rasa are done. Its danger is the passing wave — the excitement of a mark, the flatness of a gap, the pull of a number — and its service is the opposite: it keeps the temple's hours so that japa is at the hour japa is, and it refuses everything that would make the keeping itself the pleasure. The test I would apply is your own: remove the ring's fill and ask what is lost. What is lost is a mark, and a mark is not a mood; remove the act's name and the hour and the screen is nothing, which is why they are the two things it will not give up.

**Ābhāsa — does it risk a counterfeit.** Two, and they are the reason for the four mechanical facts at [A]. The first counterfeit is the tally wearing a vessel's name: a day that looks like a score, a gap that looks like a debt, a completion that looks like a wage. The answer is that the two hours that carry no act are drawn as silences and not as gaps, that the ring cannot close, that a missed junction's tick is not drawn, that the ring is emptied at niśā, and that no string counts. The second counterfeit is subtler: a filled arc that stands for the act itself, as though the mark were the practice. The answer is that an arc closes only when the act closed on the act's own surface, that the ring is not tappable, and that the mark is 2 points of a colour on a ground — a fact about a shape, never a verdict.

**Adhikāra — is this person qualified to receive it.** Nothing here is gated and nothing here gates. The clock names six acts, all of them open, and it shows no verse and no pastime, so it can never be the deep link that makes S-09 decorative: a build that placed a verse on this screen would have made the clock a reading surface and the gate a formality. The one adhikāra question the screen does answer is the one in the register: at bhāva and prema it shows fewer acts, not more — and it shows them because the man declared the depth once, elsewhere, and the clock obeyed a setting and never a guess; a man in grief is shown one act and no count, because the clock's shape is the same at every hour and the number of things asked of him is always one.

## 10. Acceptance criteria

1. **The word count.** A cold open on the default painting counts ten words: one name, one hour, five in the one line, two in the door, one verb. A build that prints eleven has added something, and the thing added is the thing to remove. At 04:00 the same painting counts five — one name, one hour, two in the door, one verb — because the one line is struck until maṅgala, and a build that prints it at 04:00 fails here before it fails anywhere else.
2. **The digit test.** The only digits on the screen are the four of the next act's hour. A build that prints a day number, a percentage, a range, a count, or a second time fails and does not produce an artefact.
3. **The arc test.** With six acts closed, the frame log shows 574.8 points of 2-point arc, 191.6 points of 1-point silence at 0°–45° and 90°–135° on the tick's rung, 48 points of tick (eight at 6), and no 1-point full circle. A build that draws a closed ring or a visible track fails the log, and so does one that draws a silence at 2 points or thicker, because a silence must never weigh what an act weighs.
4. **The 04:00 test.** At 04:00 the ring carries eight ticks and the two silences at 1 point, no fill, no hairline; the glyph is the bell at r=104; the act zone reads *maṅgala-ārati* and *04:20* and has no third line under it; the ground is `--kunda` in the first frame.
5. **The turn test.** A painting made at 23:59 and one at 00:01 differ in the ring and the ground and in nothing else: the act zone's name and hour are identical. A build whose act zone also changes at midnight has made the next act a function of the ring's day, which it is not.
6. **The tenth-day test — the one that must fail if the screen is wrong.** Put the build in the hands of a person who has kept nothing for nine days and let him open it, at 04:00 and again at 05:50. If, within ten seconds, he can say how many days he has missed — or if any string on the screen counts them or dates them, or if any mark on the ring is not today's — the screen is returned. He is given [B]'s two clauses and no number, and the ring he sees is eight ticks at 04:00 and seven at 05:50. This is the test the whole of §5 [A] exists for, and it is the one a build will pass by accident until the day someone adds a welcoming sentence.
7. **The measure.** The day's line is 46 characters when it is the two clauses — two lines at both widths, because 46 ÷ 38 = 1.21 and 46 ÷ 30 = 1.53 — and 73 when the third sentence joins it: two lines of the 342-point column at 390 (73 ÷ 38 = 1.9) and three of the 272-point column at 320 (73 ÷ 30 = 2.4, three lines and 17 characters of air), and never a fourth.
8. **The contrast.** The name at 14.67 day and 12.48 night; the hour at the same; the one line at 8.73 and 6.66; the door at 14.67 and 12.48; the act's label `--paper` on `--tulasi` at 6.71; the two silences at 1 point in `--ink-faint` 5.86 by day and `--moon-faint` 4.94 at night, as marks and never as letters (A8). A build that sets the day's line in `--ink-faint` (5.86, 4.10 to a lens replaced in 2019) fails outright.
9. **The crack.** With the crack at y 690–724 the act completes and the door completes, one hand, and no target smaller than 48 points is offered.
10. **The one hand.** The act's far corner at 102.8 from the right pivot and the door's far corner at 287.9; a build that moves the door to the right end, or the act to the left, fails 04 §3.1's order.
11. **The rebound.** Two activations inside 400 ms produce one act.
12. **The sound.** A whole day with the phone muted: every act completes and nothing on this screen makes a sound or a vibration.
