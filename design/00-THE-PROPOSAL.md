# THE FIRST SUBMISSION
## A letter to Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī, with the whole design laid on the floor before him

**Day 46 of your ninety.** The taxonomy is on the floor of your room; the gates are a one-page checklist; the ten people have been sat with. You said the next thing is not a feed but a day — the clock, the eight junctions, the silences. I am the one who has to draw it, and I would rather be told now that I have misunderstood than after eleven screens.

So this letter is not a pitch. It is the whole of what I intend to bring you, laid out at once, so that you can refuse the shape of it before I spend a month on the details.

---

### What I have taken as fixed, without asking you

There are things I did not think were mine to propose. The home is a clock and not a feed. There is no visible stage, no score, no streak, no rank. Nothing is inferred from his body. Nothing is sold. Every surface ends. I have not designed a single element that finds a way around those, because I have read §8 of the specification and I understand it is not a list of preferences but a description of what this thing is.

I have also taken your severity about words. The interface will not say *user*, *content*, *engage*, *retain*, or *rate us*. It will not say "Great job." It will not say "You're on a roll."

---

### What I am bringing you, and where I am least sure

Nine positions. Each one costs something, and I have written the cost next to it, because a design that pretends to be free is a design that has hidden its bill. They are in `00-DESIGN-BRIEF.md` §2, and I am asking you to rule on all nine before I draw a single screen — accept, refine, or refuse, in your own words.

The one I am least sure of is the second. **I have proposed that the verse is always the largest thing on any screen, and that no interface text may exceed twenty points.** It follows from your own instruction — the verse is the person, and one does not make a person small — but it produces an app whose navigation is quieter than its content, and a first-time visitor may not find the door. I do not know whether that is a threshold or a wall. You have said a gate protects the person and a wall protects the gatekeeper, and I cannot tell which one I have drawn.

The other one that keeps me up is the margin. **Fifty-six points down the left side of every screen, permanently, carrying the locator and the reviewer's mark** — the *ācārya* layer, kept where a manuscript keeps its annotations. On a small phone that is an eighth of the width, spent on something most people will not read. I think that is exactly right, and I think it is indefensible on any other grounds than yours. If you tell me the margin is vanity I will take it out, and the screens will be easier and worse.

---

### What I am not bringing you

I am not bringing a dark mode that is really a second brand. There is one night ground, because you read at eight and sleep at eight, and a person at four in the morning should not be shown a different temple.

I am not bringing an onboarding. There is no tour, no carousel, no three-step welcome. A person arrives at a threshold, and the threshold either teaches him or it does not.

I am not bringing illustrations of the pastimes. Not one. A drawn Kṛṣṇa is a counterfeit of the form, and I would be the one who made it.

And I am not bringing a prototype that pretends to be the product. The specimen in `prototype/` is paper — it shows the proportions and the silence, and nothing in it works the way the real thing will, because the real thing must work with the screen off.

---

### What I am asking of you

Four things, in this order.

**First, the nine positions.** Accept, refine, or refuse, with a sentence each. I will not draw until I have them.

**Second, the gate hour, five times.** I will bring one screen at a time, printed at full size. You hold it at arm's length. You do not need to be shown anything on a device. If a screen needs a device to be understood, it is not finished.

**Third, permission to be told I am wrong in front of the others.** Your team has said you do this slowly. I would rather be corrected slowly than flattered quickly.

**Fourth — and this is the one that matters — tell me what the last screen of the day should say.** Not the home, not the clock, not the verse. The last one. You have said the last screen is the only screen that matters, and I have designed ten screens that lead to it and I do not yet know what is on it.

---

I have not asked you for a roadmap. You do not have one, and I have read why.

I have not asked you what the competitors do. You have already classified them, and the classification was not kind.

I have not asked whether this will work. That is not a design question.

I am asking whether the shape is true before I spend a month making it beautiful.

> *— the designer, at the gate hour, with the whole of it on the floor*
