# THE REGISTER OF ELEVEN
## What each surface is for, and what it must hold true

**To** Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī · **From** the designer
**Corrected on Day 51 to agree with the eleven screens as sealed.**

Eleven submissions. This register exists so that eleven hands do not invent eleven different readings of the same book — and so that you have something to hold a screen against that is not the designer's own opinion of the screen.

The direction of the correction is one way. Where a screen you have already read and ruled on contradicts this register, the register has moved to the screen, and no screen has been moved from the register. A page you have ruled on outranks a page you have not.

Everything here is inherited from `LAHARI-spec.md`, the persona charter, and your rulings in `reviews/`. Where a surface has no *aṅga*, it says so, because a surface that cannot name its limb has to name its authority instead.

## Each surface, in four lines

### S-01 · Dehalī — the threshold

**The act.** One verse and the door, no account, no lamp.
**What must be true when it is finished.** A person who has never heard of the book, in a language he does not read, meets one verse and one door with no instruction (Seal VI). Nothing is asked of him: no account, no permission, no name, no tour, and nothing to press but the door.
**The trap.** Making the threshold beautiful enough to be a shop window. A door must be findable without a sign, and the first screen must promise exactly one act and no more.
**Who must not see it.** No one is turned away at a threshold — that is what a threshold is for. The one limit is not a person but a verse: nothing here is from the Tenth Canto, and the gate is a page away and is not advertised.

### S-02 · Nitya-sevā — the day clock

**The act.** He sees the next act and opens it.
**What must be true.** A full day is six acts and eighteen minutes — 3 + 4 + 3 + 4 + 3 + 1 — inside F1's twenty. A missed day produces zero red and one optional line. The ring is eight equal arcs and fills by the act, not by the clock (your ruling 4); the two arcs that carry no act are drawn as silences from the first frame, and the ring cannot close. No percentage and no countdown at all: the hour is a time and not an interval, and `01` §5.1 took the permission and did not use it.
**The trap.** Eight equal arcs can still be read as six-of-eight-done. The fill must not be legible as a tally — a vessel is not a scoreboard with better manners.
**Who must not see it.** At bhāva and prema the clock must show *fewer* acts, not more (R7), and it shows them because the depth was declared once elsewhere and obeyed, never guessed. A person arriving in grief must see one act, not eight.

### S-03 · Nāma — the name

**The act.** He counts a bead without looking.
**What must be true.** Sixteen rounds with the glass awake and dark — face-up, in the hand or flat on the quilt — and one press is one bead. Nothing counts aloud by default, and the numeral is the one number a person may switch off, decided before he sits at S-10. Kīrtana is not on this screen: 32 has no line the install can give it and 33 is a room, and the microphone was refused on Day 47 and does not return here by another door.
**The trap.** The bead becoming a tally; and the tick arriving when the app decides, so that a round is handed to the ear as a sound.
**Who must not see it.** A person in a room full of people must be able to chant silently, with no light and no sound, and see no number when he looks.

### S-04 · Darśana — the viewing

**The act.** He looks at the form, and offers one lamp.
**What must be true.** The form is a photograph — 294 × 441, provenance in the margin as a locator and, where a person signed, a seal. No zoom into the face, no filter, no caption over the form, no second piece of anything after it: the form stays at full while the lamp is offered, and the flame returns to the screen that has its hour. The offering is aṅga 49 and has no hour, and it notifies no one and leaves no receipt.
**The trap.** Treating the form as something to swipe through — a card, a feed item — and treating the offering as a like. Also the opposite trap: refusing the form altogether, which you corrected on Day 47.
**Who must not see it.** The form of the Deity is not gated; the *pastimes* are. The distinction must be visible on this screen, not buried in S-09.

### S-05 · Śravaṇa — the hearing

**The act.** He hears one verse, and the chapter ends.
**What must be true.** Verse at 30/42 in reading mode. Sanskrit, transliteration, rendering and the foot provenance sentence (I2) always present, never behind a tap. One-verse mode is genuinely one verse, and the chapter ends by having no next verse; the verse may be heard again, and nothing advances. A chapter end is an end.
**The trap.** The next-verse reflex; and the provenance line drifting back into the margin because the margin is prettier.
**Who must not see it.** Tenth Canto material is gated (`BRS 1.2.240`). The reader must not become the bypass that makes S-09 decorative.

### S-06 · Saṅga — the company

**The act.** He opens the room at the foot of the evening window, bows once to the person who spoke last, and reads the window up to its oldest word.
**What must be true.** Rooms of thirty or fewer, each with a named human host. Replies arrive in two daily windows. The sender of a *praṇāma* sees nothing; the recipient sees a total. No member counts, no post counts, no like counts anywhere. A death in the room closes it: the host's line carries the death, the thread opens with *The room is closed.*, the names stay, the hour is not kept, and the band holds the door alone.
**The trap.** The room becoming a feed with a name attached; the host becoming an influencer with a following.
**Who must not see it.** No messages from strangers; no one under thirteen outside family mode.

### S-07 · Smaraṇa — the remembering

**The act.** He writes one sentence to himself, or speaks one note, and seals it on the phone.
**What must be true.** Sealed on the device, unreadable by the app's own ranking. Export in plain text. Delete in one action. No autosave, no suggestions, no character count, no "continue writing". The page says where the words are and never who has read them: *Nothing you wrote has left this phone.*
**The trap.** The prompt becoming a recommendation, and the journal becoming a corpus to be mined later.
**Who must not see it.** No person reads it — not the room, not the team, not the ranking. Crisis language is handled on the device and surfaces help; it never files a report.

### S-08 · Anartha — the obstruction

**The act.** He answers four questions in his own words and writes one sentence, and nothing he writes is kept.
**What must be true.** No score, no graph, no streak, no percentile, and no week: the page keeps nothing, so it cannot know a week has passed, and it has no bell. The classical categories appear as vocabulary, not as a diagnosis. The output is a sentence he wrote, not a number the app computed.
**The trap.** The audit becoming a dashboard of the soul; and the categories hardening into a checklist, which is *niyamāgraha* with a progress bar.
**Who must not see it.** A person who has not yet begun *bhajana-kriyā*. It arrives after the practice and not before it, it is reached from the day, and nothing offers it.

### S-09 · Ācārya & the Gate — the authority and the gate

**The act.** He opens the chain behind a sentence, or he waits at a gate.
**What must be true.** Every rendered sentence resolves to book, verse, translator, reviewer, date and proofing. The gate is time and saṅga and a person who knows him — not a checkbox. The waiting room is a page you are given, not a wall you are held behind. **The app opens no path around the gate** — search, a deep link, a bookmark, a notification, a shared link, a QR, a browser, a screenshot, a festival day, thirty days offline, the room and the app's own clock all resolve here; the thirteenth path is a man's hand, a line of the opened reading remembered and typed into a thread, and no build shuts it.
**The trap.** The gate becoming a paywall or a quiz; the chain becoming a disclaimer nobody reads.
**Who must not see it.** No gated locator is reachable by any path the app opens, and the one path it cannot shut is named rather than promised shut (Seal III). You test this yourself by trying to break in.

### S-10 · The End & the Quiet — the close

**The act.** He closes, and the screen dims to one line.
**What must be true.** Every *pradakṣiṇā* ends at three rounds, and each close prints one sentence and never two: a round's end prints *The session is closed.*, and śayana at 20:30 prints *The day is finished. Nothing is asked of you.* The line names the cause and the hour gives only the ink — 8.73 by day, `--moon` 12.48 at night (I3). A bell is armed at one ārati for that ārati alone, never between 21:00 and 04:00, never on ekādaśī night. No ceiling is printed and no press declares a depth: the app applies only what is true of every man — the window and three in a day — and at the far stages the hand stops arming.
**The trap.** The close becoming a summary — a score, a "come back tomorrow", a reward. The last screen may give nothing back except the sentence.
**Who must not see it.** No one. But at bhāva and prema the whole app must recede, not merely this screen (R7).

### S-11 · System surfaces — the floor

**The act.** None. It is what holds when something fails.
**What must be true.** Thirty days offline without a lie. Errors in three plain sentences. No spinner that promises. Permissions asked at the moment of the act, never at first open. Two hundred per cent text reflows without truncation. The first open carries no string of the floor's at all: it is the day's shape, no sheet, no ask, the name one press away. Nothing in an empty state is cheerful, and an empty state states the fact and stops.
**The trap.** The error becoming an apology with a personality; the offline state becoming a nag; the empty state selling something.
**Who must not see it.** No one. This is the surface the poorest phone sees first, and the surface a bereaved person meets if the network fails at 03:40.
